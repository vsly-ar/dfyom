# Dogfood: the user's 100 mm plate, end to end over the agent CLI (0.4.238)

Status: draft (dogfood report, 2026-11-28) · Source: the user's job of TG #492 and the DF144–DF147 intake rows in [hardening.md](../plans/hardening.md#intake-user-feedback-goes-here) · API: [llm-api.md](../architecture/llm-api.md) · Features: [design.md § Facing region](../modes/design.md#facing-region-hardening-df145), [features.md § Clone groups in Plan](../modes/features.md#clone-groups-in-plan-hardening-df146), [engraved-patterns.md § Simple Line](../modes/engraved-patterns.md#simple-line-straight-and-arc-hardening-df147) · Findings → DF149–DF161

**What was run.** The user's job, rebuilt with the tools shipped for it, headless through `dfyom batch` (the built `out/main/cli.js`, worktree at 0.4.238) on a **copy** of the user's project (`plate-copy.dfyom` copied again; nothing under the user's projects or library was opened). No window opened. The user's library was out of reach, so a scratch `--data-dir` got the machine profile ("3020 Nova") and the five tools rebuilt from the project's own snapshots.

**The job (TG #492).** A 100 × 60 × 10.1 mm 6061 plate. Face only a 22 mm band along the front edge (0.2 mm, the 1/4" 3F finishing flat). Four M6 clearance holes (Ø6.6) with 90° countersinks (Ø13.84 at the face): the 1/4" 1F flat (Ø6.22) drills first, the 1/4" 90° V-bit cuts the countersink, one hole cloned ×3 at 20 mm. A straight through Line cuts the 22 mm part off with the 1/4" flat. A 1 mm one-sided chamfer goes on the part's wall only, with the V-bit. The user's stop block (left of the stock, top Z−0.6) is the only workholding the project models.

**Reading of the brief.** The kept part is the 22 mm strip in front of the Line: the band, the four holes and the chamfered wall are all on it. The Line's centreline is at `22 + 6.22/2` so the part is 22 mm wide (see F8). The archived design in the file differs (M5 holes at Y17, a slot pocket at Y0.5–7). The brief's M6 and 22 mm were used.

**Time.** Facing 0.5 min, drilling 2.2 min, countersinks 3.1 min, cut-off 9.9 min (35 passes of 0.311 mm, the calculator's slotting stepdown on an extrusion frame), chamfer 0.2 min; total 16 min. The export estimate is within 2 % of the virtual run.

**Toolpaths as cut.** The band: five climb rows along X, plunged in the air past the right end, the tool's edge on the band's inner side at Y22.0, no boundary pass (a straight inner side along the rows). The Line: every pass plunges in the air at X−5.11 or X105.11 and alternates; 10.6 mm = 10.1 + 0.5 breakthrough. The chamfer: one V-bit ring at Y22, tip Z−1, entered from the air. The countersinks: helical rings on the hole's edge, with the DF142 outer rings at two upper levels.

**Would it be safe to cut?** The toolpaths are: every oracle is clean and the virtual machine reaches Idle. The job as planned is not, for three reasons outside the toolpaths, all warned or visible: (1) the profile's Z headroom says no tool reaches the stock (F2): measure `noseToTable` first; (2) the model holds neither side of the cut-off (`cutOffLoose`), and the chamfer then runs on the freed part (F4): clamp or tape the 22 mm strip; (3) the V-bit's re-zero for the chamfer is on the offcut after the cut-off (F3): touch off on the band instead. With those three handled it is safe to cut.

**Also seen, already on record.** A retried `run.job.continue { toolChange: 2 }` that arrives late could end the _next_ T2 change. The host compares the tool number only, so [run-v0.1.md decision 9](../modes/run-v0.1.md#decisions) ("a retried call that names its wait never ends the next one") does not hold when a tool comes back. **What worked well.** The band is one field and faces exactly the band: rows to the inner side, open sides off the stock. Clone groups are one plan operation each, labelled "× 4". The Straight Line enters every pass from the air and the chamfer side is right. The countersink cone is exact with a 1/4" V-bit (DF142 levels). The hint on a wrong argument (`timeout` → "did you mean `timeoutSeconds`?") saved a round trip. `job.wait` with `next` drives the tool changes without reading docs. The estimate is within 2 %.
