# Engraved patterns and the Line operation

Status: accepted (by delegation, 2026-11-25: the user delegated the choices, D70, so the recommended options below are **accepted by delegation** and the user may veto; hardening [DF112, DF113](../plans/hardening.md#intake-user-feedback-goes-here), user TG #352; E0 spike run 2026-11-25, [report](../research/2026-11-25-engraved-patterns-and-lines.md#spike-e0)) · Research: [engraved patterns and lines](../research/2026-11-25-engraved-patterns-and-lines.md) · Builds on: [patterns.md](patterns.md) (lattices, boundary, presets dialog), [edge-chamfers.md](edge-chamfers.md) (V-bit toolpaths), [design-v0.3.md](design-v0.3.md) (shapes, open paths, SVG import), [features.md](features.md) (feature model A1–A6), [geometry.md § Carving (later)](../architecture/geometry.md#carving-later) (engraving on-line) · Terms: [glossary.md](../glossary.md)

The user asked (TG #352): "add a new feature: an engraved pattern and an operation it will need: a Line. The Line may be curved and have different depth across its path. I guess the easiest way to have this flexibility and configurability would be by SVGs (where depth is encoded in color or brightness) … please also create some typical presets with SVGs. And also create a few circular ones please (for a round surface)."

- A **Line** is an operation. It cuts a groove along the centreline of open or closed paths, and its depth may change along the path. It is candidate 9 of [features.md § Candidate features](features.md#candidate-features) and the "Engraving (on-line)" of [geometry.md § Carving](../architecture/geometry.md#carving-and-engraving-vector-based).
- An **engraved pattern** is a feature. It places a **motif** (a preset SVG, an imported SVG or the part's shapes) once or on a lattice over an area, and cuts it with one owned Line.

## Line operation

`design.line.add` creates an operation of kind `line`:

| Field   | Kind / values                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Default                                               |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| `on`    | Face target ([design-v0.2.md § Face targets](design-v0.2.md#face-targets-generalised-on)), as a pocket                                                                                                                                          | Last facing's floor, else `stock.top`                 |
| `paths` | 1–50 placed named shapes `{ shapeId, center, rotation?, mirror?, scale? }`, as a pocket's named shape. Every loop and open path of each shape is a line. Or (DF147 SL2) one inline `{ segment }` / `{ arc }`, [§ Simple Line](#geometry-modes)  | –                                                     |
| `depth` | A length (constant), `{ start, end }` (linear along each line, by arc length), or `{ min, max }` (from the shapes' [levels](#levels-on-shapes): level 0 → `min`, 1 → `max`; a path without levels is level 1)                                   | `{ min: 0.2 mm, max: 1 mm }` with levels, else 0.5 mm |
| `blend` | Length ≥ 0: a level step is blended over ±`blend` along the line                                                                                                                                                                                | 1 mm                                                  |
| `tool`  | The tool field (A4 as on other operations): `chamferMill` (V-bit, the default auto choice), `taperedBallEndMill` (library only, ranked after V-bits, [tapered-ball-nose.md](tapered-ball-nose.md#line-operation)), `ballEndMill`, `flatEndMill` | auto: a 60° V-bit                                     |
| `name`  | Default "Line 1"                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |                                                       |

- **Plan strategy keys** (plan-only, as other operations): `stepdown` (from feeds), `entry` (`ramp` default, `plunge`: V-bits and tapered ball end mills only), `rampAngle` (0.5°–30°, default 10°, the chamfer's range).
- **Surfaces:** none. A groove's floor is not a face target.
- **Invariants:** every depth > 0 and less than the stock under the face (`lessThanStockThickness`); `min ≤ max`; `{ start, end }` > 0; the shapes exist (`unknownShape`). A shape of only closed loops is fine (a closed line). Inline paths: `inlineAlone`, `lineTooShort`, `arcCollinear` ([Implementation (SL1–SL2)](#implementation-sl1sl2)).
- **Removal solid** (built otherwise, see [Implementation (E1)](#implementation-e1)): the tool swept along each line, built per chord as the convex hull of the tool solid (cone, ball-capped cylinder, cylinder, ball under a cone for a tapered ball) at the chord's two ends, unioned per line. Arcs are chorded at the surface tolerance. Built and cached per line. E1 measures the cost on the rosette (≈ 1 400 mm of line) against the evaluation budget; per-cell batching as in [patterns.md § Chamfer](patterns.md#chamfer) is the fallback.
- **Width read-out:** the groove width at a depth: V-bit w = dt + 2 d tan(α/2); ball 2 √(d (D − d)) up to D; flat D ([research § Tools](../research/2026-11-25-engraved-patterns-and-lines.md#tools-entries-and-passes)); tapered ball the ball, then its tangent cone ([tapered-ball-nose.md § The tool](tapered-ball-nose.md#the-tool)).

### Levels on shapes

- A `path` item ([design-v0.3.md § Shape definition](design-v0.3.md#shape-definition)) gains optional `levels`: one number 0..1 for the whole path, or one per vertex (vertex 0 = start, i = end of segment i; `levels.length` = segments + 1, closed paths repeat vertex 0 at the end). The level is linear by arc length between vertices. Rules: `levelRange` (0..1) and `levelCount`.
- **Booleans ignore levels.** A shape with levels used by a pocket or profile cuts as before. `dfm.levelsIgnored` (info) says so when levels exist but the operation is not a Line or the Line's depth is constant.
- **Shape Editor:** a path with levels draws in a dark-to-light ramp (the same as its SVG preview), and each vertex has a Level field. A "Levels" toggle in the toolbar shows or hides the ramp.
- **No migration:** an absent field is the old behaviour (pre-alpha rule; `schemaVersion` unchanged).

### SVG encoding

How `design.shape.import { levels }` reads depth (default `colour`; `width`; `none`). The research compares the options ([§ Encoding depth](../research/2026-11-25-engraved-patterns-and-lines.md#encoding-depth-in-an-svg)).

1. **`data-level`** on an element (or inherited from its group): one value 0..1, or stops spread evenly from start to end (`"0 1 0"` fades in and out). The importer splits elements at the stops' positions, so the vertex levels are exact. This wins over the colour.
2. **Stroke colour** (`stroke` attribute or `style`, inherited): level = 1 − luma (Rec. 709) of the opaque colour. Black is the deepest; white strokes are refused as invisible (`levelWhite`, warning). Fills are ignored for open paths; a closed path's level comes from its stroke, else from its fill.
3. **Colour legend:** the import dialog lists every distinct colour found with its element count and default level, and each level can be edited before import. That is how a red/blue drawing becomes 1 mm / 0.3 mm. The result stores the levels, not the colours.
4. **`width`:** level = stroke width (after the transform) ÷ the widest stroke, for V-carve-style drawings.
5. **Chaining** is done by the Line, not the importer. Open paths of one Line whose ends meet within 0.01 mm form one line, and level steps between them are blended.
6. **Ignored, with a report warning:** `stroke-opacity`/`opacity` (`opacityIgnored`), gradient strokes (`strokeGradientIgnored`; a linear or radial gradient as a spatial ramp is E5), Inkscape Power Stroke outlines (they are fills).

The importer's `SvgShape` gains the element's stroke paint and width per item. The XML walk already has them ([`import.ts`](../../src/core/import/svg/import.ts) reads `style`). The import report gains `levels: { mode, colours: [{ colour, count, level }] }`.

## Toolpath (Plan)

A new generator `generateLine` (`src/core/toolpath/line.ts`), request kind `line`. The E0 spike's [`line.ts`](../../spikes/engrave/line.ts) is its sketch.

- **Profile:** per line, the depth along its arc length s as piecewise-linear z(s): levels mapped by `{ min, max }`, steps blended over ±`blend` (clamped to half the neighbouring pieces), measured from the `on` face.
- **Passes:** n = ⌈max depth / stepdown⌉. Pass k cuts z_k(s) = max(z(s), −k · stepdown), and the last pass is the profile. Open lines alternate direction (no return rapid); closed lines keep climb direction (as profiles) and start at the middle of their longest element (the edge chamfer's rule).
- **Entry (ramp-back):** before pass k, go `Lr` ahead in the pass direction along the floor already cut (pass 1: rapid, then feed down to the face), then ramp back to the pass start reaching z_k(start), at most `rampAngle`, never below z_k. Lr = drop / tan(rampAngle), capped at the line length. A line too short for the ramp zigzags on itself. Closed lines ramp along the loop before the start, as the chamfer's `rampRing`. `entry: 'plunge'` (V-bits, tapered ball end mills) feeds straight down at `plungeRate`.
- **Moves:** `G1` with Z for lines; arcs with a linear Z are helical `G2`/`G3`; arcs are split at 90° and at every profile breakpoint. A profile that is not linear on an arc is split there.
- **Order:** lines in nearest-neighbour order from the front-left, each line entered at its nearer end (open lines are reversible). All passes of a line before the next. Travel uses [safe links](safe-links.md).
- **Crossings:** cut as they come (the later line cuts air in the earlier groove). Their count shows in the read-out. **Coincident** segments are removed before the toolpath (the deeper kept).
- **Feeds:** V-bits use [F10](../architecture/feeds-speeds.md#chamfer-mills-v04) with De = dt + 2 × max depth × tan(α/2), engagement `chamfer { depth }`. Tapered ball end mills use [F11](../architecture/feeds-speeds.md#tapered-ball-end-mills-hardening-df124), De = the groove width at the max depth, engagement `taper { depth }`, with a suggested stepdown. Ball and flat end mills use a full-width slot at the pass depth.
- **Spike numbers:** a 91 mm three-colour line, 0.52–1.5 mm deep, came to 2 passes, a 4.5 mm ramp, 36 gCode lines and 189 mm of cut ([research § Spike E0](../research/2026-11-25-engraved-patterns-and-lines.md#spike-e0)).

## Engraved pattern (feature)

`design.engraving.add` creates a feature of kind `engraving` (A1–A6 unchanged):

| Field      | Kind / values                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Default                                                  |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| `on`       | Face target, as a pocket                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Last facing's floor, else `stock.top`                    |
| `motif`    | `{ preset }` (an id of [Presets](#presets)) or `{ shapeIds }` (named shapes of the part, open or closed, with levels; their joint bounding box is the motif's frame)                                                       | `{ preset: 'waves' }`                                    |
| `size`     | The motif's width (height keeps the aspect), or `scale`; with `layout: 'single'` and `fit: 'boundary'` it is derived                                                                                                       | the preset's size                                        |
| `rotation` | Angle, or `'radial'` on a polar lattice (each tile turned to face the centre)                                                                                                                                              | 0                                                        |
| `layout`   | `single` (one motif at `center`, or fitted) or a lattice: Pattern's `lattice`, `spacing` (gap ≥ 0 or pitch; touching tiles are the normal case), `angle`, `origin`, `alternate` ([patterns.md § Model](patterns.md#model)) | the preset's                                             |
| `boundary` | `face` or `{ shapeId, center, rotation? }`, as Pattern                                                                                                                                                                     | `face`                                                   |
| `margin`   | Length ≥ 0                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | 3 mm                                                     |
| `fit`      | `whole` (tiles wholly inside), `clip` (lines cut at the boundary less the margin) or, with `single`, `boundary` (the motif scaled to fit the boundary less the margin, centred)                                            | `clip` for lattices, `boundary` for single round presets |
| `depth`    | As the Line's (`{ min, max }`, constant, `{ start, end }`)                                                                                                                                                                 | `{ min: 0.2 mm, max: 1 mm }`                             |
| `name`     | Default "Engraving 1"                                                                                                                                                                                                      |                                                          |

- **Owned shape** `<id>_lines` (read-only, A1 as Pattern's `<id>_cells`) with one `engraving` item. It resolves in the 2D layer as follows:
  1. Resolve the motif: the preset SVG through the importer, or the shapes. Levels come along.
  2. Place it singly or at each lattice node (shared lattice code with the `pattern` item).
  3. Keep whole tiles or clip the lines to the boundary less the margin. Clipping an open path at a region's boundary is a new 2D port operation: split at the crossings and keep the inside pieces.
  4. Remove coincident segments, keeping the higher level.
  5. Chain lines whose ends meet across tiles.

  The resolution is cached by the item hash, as patterns.

- **Owned operation** `<id>_line`: a Line on `<id>_lines` with the feature's `on`, `depth` and its own tool field.
- **Invariants:** a resolved element count ≤ 20 000 (the SVG import limit, `engravingTooMany`); the motif has at least one path (`engravingEmpty`); `fit: 'boundary'` only with `single`; `rotation: 'radial'` only with `polar`.
- **Clones and split parts:** as patterns ([patterns.md § Split parts and Clones](patterns.md#split-parts-and-clones)). Lines crossing a split line are clipped per piece.

### Round parts

The user's "circular ones (for a round surface)" are motifs for a **round flat face**: a coaster, a lid, the top of a turned box. Two ways:

- **A round preset, single, fitted:** pick Rosette, set the boundary to the part's round outline (a circle shape or the outline profile's shape), and `fit: 'boundary'` scales it to the diameter less the margin.
- **A polar lattice with radial rotation:** a small motif (a leaf, a scale) repeated on rings about the centre, each turned to face outwards. Radial rotation is new here; Pattern's polar keeps cell orientation ([patterns.md § Model](patterns.md#model)) and can adopt it later.

Engraving follows flat faces only. A curved surface would need surface probing or a rotary axis, and is not planned.

## UI

Follows [ui.md § Features (v0.4)](../conventions/ui.md#features-v04) and the inspector layout of [inspector-layout.md](../conventions/inspector-layout.md).

- **Add list:** "Line" among the operations; "Engraved pattern" next to Pattern. With Pattern there are now two decorative features, and the Add list's grouping follows DF98's one-list rule.
- **Line inspector:** Paths (`on`, then the Geometry row Straight / Arc / Shape of [§ Simple Line](#geometry-modes); Shape: a multi-pick of shapes with New shape… / Edit shape…), Depth (a segmented choice: Constant / Start → end / From levels; then the fields), Tool, More (blend). Under the depth fields, a **legend**: a dark-to-light bar from `max` to `min` with the levels in use as ticks.
- **Engraved pattern inspector:** Motif (Presets… button, or the part's shapes), Layout (Single / lattice tiles, as Pattern), Area (boundary, margin, fit), Depth (legend as above), Read-out.
- **Import SVG dialog:** a Levels choice (From colour, From stroke width, None). With From colour it shows the colour legend: swatch, element count, a level field per colour, and a preview of the drawing in levels.
- **Viewport:** while editing, lines are drawn at their groove width with depth as shading. The boundary less the margin is dashed and clipped-away parts are ghosts, as Pattern.
- **Plan:** the Line's plan operation shows passes and the ramp in the toolpath view like other operations. The strategy row has Entry and Ramp angle.

Same 1 428 mm: **0.53 s** for the evaluation (140 meshes, 8 240 triangles, built in 17 ms). Volumes within 1 % of the swept-cone integral (0.2 → 1.5 mm curve), a closed square V-groove, a ball and a flat groove. Per-cell batching was not needed.

## E4 follow-ups: depth legend, crossings, split pieces

Built 2026-11-26 (provisional, on a branch). Design notes first, then what was built. Code: `src/core/design/engraving/legend.ts`, `src/core/geometry/line-crossings.ts`, `clipWithin` in `src/core/geometry/line.ts`, the `line` case of `mapOperation` in `src/core/design/split/pieces.ts`, `DepthLegend.tsx`, `lineMergeLoops` in `src/renderer/src/modes/design/outline.ts`.

### Depth legend

- **What it shows:** under the Deepest and Shallowest fields of a Line or an engraved pattern with depth "From SVG levels" (only then: a constant or start → end depth has no levels), a bar 8 px high from Deepest at the left to Shallowest at the right. Deep is full ink (`--text`), shallow faint (`--text` at 12 %), as the preset thumbnails draw levels by stroke opacity, so dark = deep in both themes' sense of "more ink". Neutral only, no accent. The two depths are under the bar's ends (`--text-xs`, tabular figures).
- **Ticks:** one 1 px tick (`--text-muted`) per level in use, at x = (1 − level) of the bar. Levels within 0.01 are one tick. A path without levels and a primitive are level 1; an open path's per-vertex levels each count; an engraved pattern's owned shape counts its resolved lines' levels. More than 12 distinct levels (a continuous fade, Wood grain) draws one band over the range instead of a comb of ticks.
- **Name:** the bar is `role="img"`; its accessible name reads "Depth legend: dark is deep, 1 mm; light is shallow, 0.2 mm. Depths in use: 1 mm, 0.68 mm, 0.48 mm." (or "The depth fades from … to …").
- **Not here:** the import dialog's colour legend (E2's deferred items) is a different legend (colour → level, before import).

### Crossings and groove width

- **Crossings** are the places where two grooves cross or meet (the tool passes twice; the later cuts air in the earlier, § Toolpath): exact element intersections between the resolved lines and between non-neighbouring elements of one line, a T-junction counting as a meeting; neighbours along a line and a loop's closing joint do not count; points within 0.01 mm are one place. Bucketed on a grid of element boxes: a 20 × 20 grid of lines (400 crossings) or a tiled lid of thousands of arcs stays in milliseconds; cached per resolution.
- **Read-out:** "209 tiles · 19 lines · 10 mm wide · 2038 mm of groove · grooves ≤ 1.15 mm wide · 12 crossings": the width is the owned Line's tool at the deepest depth (`grooveWidth`), the crossings are left out when there are none. `design.engraving.add/update` results add `crossings`, and `maxWidth` since [dryRun and maxWidth](#dryrun-and-maxwidth).

### Merge rings in the viewport

Selecting an engraved pattern (or its Line, or any Line) draws its grooves and a dashed ring (1.5 mm, as the pattern's thin-web ring) at each place `dfm.lineMerge` names (`params.points`, up to 10), and the viewport's text summary says "3 thin walls between grooves are ringed."

### Split pieces

- **Rule** (as [patterns.md § Split parts and Clones](patterns.md#split-parts-and-clones), "lines crossing a split line are clipped per piece"): a Line whose grooves lie in one cell is copied, its paths shifted by the piece's offset. A Line crossing the cell gets `within`, the cell in the piece's face frame **grown by the groove's half width** at the Line's deepest; its resolved lines are cut there after chaining and blends (`clipWithin`), so the depth along what is left is exactly the source's (a ramp does not restart; levels and blends stay). The growth matters: a groove whose centreline lies just past the seam still cuts into this piece, and the reassembly test fails without it. What lies past the cell is in the blank's waste beside the outline.
- **Model:** `within?: { min, max }` on the Line record (piece face frame), written only by the split, like the chamfer's `sourceEdge`; optional, no `schemaVersion` change. An engraved pattern's owned shape `<id>_lines` stays in a piece that carries its Line (the piece has no features; the Line places the shape at [0, 0] shifted). Shape-line cells (4e) cut at the cut's bounds; the outline trims the rest.
- **Levels:** a Line that a split level cuts (it starts above the piece's top or reaches its bottom) is left out with the split's `cutsFeature` finding ("… is a Line cut by a split level"); a Line on the piece's own top or bottom face maps like a pocket.
- **Tests:** `src/core/design/split/reassembly.test.ts` (a ramped open line, a ramped loop and an engraved pattern across the panel's line: points beside and below the grooves near the seam are in the source ⇔ in exactly one piece), `src/core/geometry/line-crossings.test.ts`, the crossings in `tiling.test.ts`, `src/core/design/engraving/legend.test.ts`, `DepthLegend.test.tsx`, the merge rings in `outline.test.ts`. Goldens unchanged (byte-equal).

## dryRun and maxWidth

Built 2026-11-26 (provisional, on a branch): the last deferred item of the engraving plan. Code: `finish` and `FeatureCommandSpec.dryRun` in `src/core/design/feature-commands.ts`, `engravingResult` in `src/core/design/engraving/engraving.ts`, `lineCutter` moved to `src/core/design/tool-choice.ts`.

- **`dryRun: true`** on `design.engraving.add` / `.update` runs the whole command (parsing, invariants, expansion, the whole-part check, recalculation of dependants, warnings) and returns the same result, but writes the owned operations, shapes and feature record to a copy of the part, not the draft. The draft has no patches, so the dispatcher makes no commit: no undo entry, no revision bump, no pending change. Refusals are the real call's (the same code, issues and data). Stale-write and `expectedRevision` checks apply as to any document command.
- **Decided here:** Pattern's `dryRun` was not built either ([patterns.md](patterns.md) lists it as not built), so there was nothing to follow; the mechanism is generic and opt-in per feature kind (`dryRun: true` on the kind's command spec), on for the engraved pattern only. It costs one arg per tool (78 chars each), so Pattern and other kinds take it when asked. A dry run is still a document command (not a query): it reads the same draft, and its arguments are logged like other calls.
- **`maxWidth`** (mm, 0.01): the owned Line's groove at its deepest, `grooveWidth(lineCutter(line), lineDeepest(depth))`, the read-out's "grooves ≤ … mm wide". The result builder (`resultOf`) now also gets the owned operations and the document's snapshots, so a specific tool's snapshot resolves. With no tool the default 60° V-bit counts (1 mm deep: 1.15 mm).
- **Agent tools:** `dryRun` ("true: result only, part unchanged") on both tools; `maxWidth` is in the output schema only. Measured: `design_engraving_add` 1,555 → 1,633, `design_engraving_update` 1,130 → 1,208 chars; tools/list 115,800 → **115,956** (+156, 150 tools), 4.0 K headroom under 120 K.
- **Tests:** `src/core/design/engraving/tiling.test.ts` § dryRun and maxWidth: a dry add and a dry update return results equal to the real calls that follow, with the document object, revision and undo depth unchanged; dry refusals (layout, radial, unknown feature, too many elements on add) equal the real ones; `maxWidth` 1.15 mm at 1 mm and 2.31 mm at 2 mm with the default V-bit.

## Decided here (not questions)

- **Profile `on` is not extended** (EP-A). It would grow `design.profile.*`, the largest design tools (3.6 K + 3.0 K), and give one loop per operation. The Line keeps grooves of many paths in one operation.
- **Levels, not depths, on shapes** (EP-B). The same SVG serves a 0.5 mm and a 2 mm engraving. Colours are not kept after import (the legend maps them once).
- **No double-cut avoidance at crossings** (as every CAM surveyed). Coincident segments are removed because tiling makes them systematically.
- **Tiles may touch** (gap ≥ 0, no overlap invariant): grooves crossing is a design choice for lines, unlike pockets.
- **Presets are immutable once shipped** (EP-G). Projects store the preset id, and the resolution reads the shipped file. Changing a file would silently change projects.
- **The "round surface" reading** (EP-F) is taken by delegation. If the user meant a cylinder, it is a rotary-axis project and outside this doc.

## Simple Line: straight and arc (hardening DF147)

Status: **accepted** (2026-11-28, from the user's TG #492; Q235–Q238 taken as recommended by delegation D89) · Intake: [hardening.md DF147](../plans/hardening.md#intake-user-feedback-goes-here)

The user: "I think I need a 'Straight Line' operation: I'd use it to cut out the part and to chamfer only the part that stays. Current 'Line' operation is too complicated for a simple straight line, requiring to create a shape in a separate shapes editor. Or better yet, the Line op can be simplified by default for just one straight line or an arc. And user may switch to the Shape mode to be able to use a shape or SVG. Btw. there's no option to open the Shapes editor from the 'Path' dropdown (for the Line op) or anywhere on this side panel, which makes it obscure."

### Geometry modes

- **Proposal:** the [Line operation](#line-operation) keeps its kind and gains an inline geometry. A `paths` item is either a placed named shape (as now: **Shape** mode) or one inline element on the `on` face, in its face frame:
  - **Straight:** `{ segment: { start: [u, v], end: [u, v] } }`.
  - **Arc:** `{ arc: { start, through, end } }` (three points, Q236); the inspector shows its radius and sweep.
- **Default:** Add → Line opens in **Straight** mode with a line across the face's middle; the inspector's first row is a segmented choice **Straight / Arc / Shape**. Straight and Arc hold one element; Shape is today's multi-pick (and Import SVG…). Switching Straight/Arc → Shape offers "Convert to shape", which creates a named shape from the element so nothing is lost; Shape → Straight keeps the shape in the library.
- **Coordinates** are quantities and may be expressions (`stock.side[1].x - 22`, the right stock face's x less 22). **Decided here:** a "Parallel to an edge" helper (edge picker + distance) fills a Straight line across the whole face at that distance from the edge: "22 mm from the front edge".
- **Everything after the geometry is the Line's:** depth (constant, start → end; levels need a shape), tool, passes, ramp-back, toolpath, removal solid. An inline element is turned into one open path before geometry and the toolpath, so the generators do not change.
- **Shape Editor access (decided here):** the Path picker of the Line, and every other shape picker (pocket, profile, Clones `along`), lists **New shape…** and **Edit shape…** (the picked one); the Line panel gets an "Edit in Shape Editor" button in Shape mode. A shape made by New shape… is picked when the editor closes.

### Cutting the part out along a line

- **Through Line (Q237):** `depth: 'through'` (stock extent under the face plus the profile's `breakthrough`), a flat end mill, the slot as wide as the tool (a wider slot is a Profile `on`, [design-v0.3.md § Cut side and open shapes](design-v0.3.md#cut-side-and-open-shapes)). The `lessThanStockThickness` rule keeps holding for every other depth.
- **Ends on the stock edge:** an end on or beyond a stock edge is an **open end**: passes enter from outside the stock at full level depth, as facing rows do (no ramp needed); an end inside the face keeps ramp-back.
- **Release:** a through Line that splits the stock is ordered as a through profile (after the operations on both sides, the freeing cut last) and takes the loose-piece check; no tabs in this slice (a straight cut-off frees a bar end, not a held part).

### One-sided chamfer along a line

- **Proposal (Q238):** a [chamfer](edge-chamfers.md#model) may take `edgeOf` a Line or a profile `on`, with a new `edgeSide: 'left' | 'right'` (relative to the path from start to end) naming **the side that stays**. The chamfered edge is the groove's wall at the face on that side: the path offset by half the groove width at the face, clipped to the stock face; round ends are not chamfered. It is cut by the edge chamfer's toolpath ([edge-chamfers.md § Toolpath](edge-chamfers.md#toolpath-plan)).
- **UI:** Chamfer in the Line row's menu; the inspector shows "Side that stays: Left / Right" with an arrow drawn on the line in the viewport, and clicking either side of the line picks it.

### Questions (Q235–Q238)

1. **Q235 – Where the simple line lives.** (1) **The Line operation with Straight / Arc / Shape modes, Straight by default** (above). (2) A separate "Straight line" operation kind. (3) Shapes only, with a quicker New shape… in the picker. _Recommendation: (1). One kind keeps one tool, one toolpath and one agent tool; the user's second suggestion._
2. **Q236 – How an arc is given.** (1) **Start, a point it passes through, end** (any sweep below 360°, no small/large ambiguity, snaps to points). (2) Centre, radius, start and end angles. (3) Start, end, radius and direction (G-code style). _Recommendation: (1), with radius and sweep shown read-only._
3. **Q237 – Cutting out along a line.** (1) **A through depth on the Line: flat end mill, slot = tool width, open ends enter from outside the stock, freeing-cut order and loose-piece check, no tabs.** (2) No Line change; Profile `on` gets the inline Straight / Arc geometry instead. (3) Both. _Recommendation: (1). The user asked for the Line; a wider slot or tabs remain Profile `on`'s job._
4. **Q238 – Chamfering only the side that stays.** (1) **`edgeOf` a Line or a profile `on` with `edgeSide` left/right on the chamfer, picked as "the side that stays".** (2) A `keep` side on the Line, which a chamfer and the plan read. (3) A chamfer option on the Line itself. _Recommendation: (1). It reuses the edge chamfer and lifts edge-chamfers.md's "`on` profiles: later" for both kinds._

### Implementation (SL1–SL2)

- **SL1 (built):** the Line's Path picker lists, in a "Shape Editor" group, **New shape…** (adds a shape of one open straight path, 40 mm / 1.5" along u, makes it that path's shape at the same position and opens the [Shape Editor window](../conventions/ui.md#shape-editor-window-df94): two commands, one undo step) and **Edit shape…** (the picked shape); an **Edit in Shape Editor** button sits under each path's picker. The pocket and profile picker (which already had New shape…) gains **Edit shape…** for a named shape. The Shape Editor is its own window, so the project window keeps the operation selected; the new shape is already picked when the editor opens. **Decided here:** the Clones `along` and engraved-pattern motif pickers are not changed in SL1 (the motif picker has its own Presets…/Shapes flow; `along` has no picker in the inspector yet). Tests: `LineShape.test.tsx` (with axe).
- **SL2 (built):** inline geometry on the Line.
  - **Model:** a `paths` item is a placed named shape, `{ segment: { start, end } }` or `{ arc: { start, through, end } }` (points in the `on` face's frame, quantities, formulas allowed: `stock.side[1].x - 22`). Optional in the record: no `schemaVersion` change. The mode is read from the paths (no stored mode). Parameter paths follow the record: `paths.0.segment.start.0`, `paths.0.arc.through.1`; Clones move and mirror those points, a split piece shifts them.
  - **Invariants:** `inlineAlone` (an inline item is the Line's only path, at `paths.<i>`), `lineTooShort` (end ≥ 0.01 mm from start, at `paths.<i>.<kind>.end`), `arcCollinear` (`through` off the straight start → end, at `paths.<i>.arc.through`).
  - **Resolution:** [`line-inline.ts`](../../src/core/design/line-inline.ts) turns the item into one open path placed as a named shape's (relative to its bounding box's centre), so the Line's generators, removal solid and plan are unchanged. A Straight Line 22 mm from the front edge and a three-point arc export the same motion as the same lines drawn as shapes, and as the arc after Convert to shape (`line-inline-plan.test.ts`).
  - **Convert to shape:** `design.shape.convert` takes a Line in Straight / Arc mode: a new shape of one open path from [0, 0] (an arc's radius rounded up to 0.1 µm), placed at the element's box centre; one undo step.
  - **Inspector:** a segmented **Geometry** row Straight / Arc / Shape first under `on`. Straight: Start u/v, End u/v, a "Length" read-out, **Parallel to** (edge: front v = 0, back, left u = 0, right, in the face frame) and **Distance from edge** behind More; committing the distance (or changing the edge after one) places a straight line across the whole face, values written as numbers. Arc: Start, Through, End and a "Radius …, sweep …°" read-out. Both show **Convert to shape**. **Decided here:** choosing Shape runs Convert to shape (nothing is lost; the Path picker then offers the other shapes); Shape → Straight places the line across the face's middle and keeps the shape in the library; Straight → Arc keeps the ends and puts `through` left of the chord by a quarter of its length; Arc → Straight drops `through`. The plan copy cannot switch to Shape (it has no convert).
  - **Add → Line** opens in Straight across the face's middle along u (the old wave shape is gone).
  - **Agent API:** `paths` items of `design.line.add` accept `{ segment }` / `{ arc }`; `design.shape.convert` names Lines. `tools/list` measured 111,772 → 112,279 chars (+0.5 K, above the 0.3 K estimate; within the 120 K budget).
  - **Not built (decided here):** viewport handles for the endpoints (the fields and Parallel to cover SL2's flows; handles wait for a dogfood need).
  - Tests: `line-inline.test.ts` (geometry, commands, invariants), `line-inline-plan.test.ts` (byte-equal motion), `LineShape.test.tsx` (modes, Parallel to, convert, axe), `e2e/line.e2e.ts` (Straight → Plan, New shape… from the picker).

### Implementation (SL3–SL4)

- **SL3 (built):** the through Line.
  - **Model:** `depth: 'through'` (DTO literal, `design.line.add/update` arg); no `schemaVersion` change. Invariant `lineThroughPath` (at `depth`): a through Line is one inline Straight or Arc; a cut-out along a shape is a Profile (with tabs). The inspector's Depth row offers **Through** outside Shape mode; it replaces the depth fields with a note. Engraved patterns refuse it (a bad length).
  - **Geometry:** the depth is the material below `on` (the face's z plus the stock's extent in its direction); the geometry carries `through: true`; the removal solid is the slot swept by the flat cutter past the bottom, role `cutOff`. **Decided here:** with an automatic or non-flat tool the design shows a Ø6 flat (`DEFAULT_THROUGH_CUTTER`); the plan's tool fit refuses a non-flat tool ("a through Line needs a flat end mill") and the auto choice takes end mills only. A piece the cut-off frees gets its own `dfm.loosePiece` text ("hold both sides of the line").
  - **Plan:** the line strategy takes `breakthrough` (shown only for a through Line; the profile's default); the toolpath depth is material + breakthrough and the reach check / minimum stickout count it. DF50's leftover `throughDepth` is not applied to Lines. Every through Line is a **freeing cut**: it comes after the non-freeing cuts of its face (`releaseProfiles`), its own chamfers after it; an operation planned after it gets `plan.loosePiece` (rule `cutOffFirst`), a later setup `plan.cutoutBeforeSetup`. The split check counts it through.
  - **Toolpath:** an end is **open** when it lies on or past the face rectangle and a straight lead along its tangent clears the stock by R + 2 mm (`openLead`). The line is entered at an open end (reversed if only its end is open), led out past each open end, and every pass at an open end plunges in the air outside the stock to the pass depth (no ramp, no plunge into material); passes alternate; an inner end keeps ramp-back. No finishing pass (the slot is the tool's width).
  - **Workholding:** `plan.workholding.cutOffLoose` (warning) per setup without a surface hold: the cut-off splits the face into the sides left / right of the line (`cut-off.ts`); each side no screw, toe clamp or vise holds is named ("the piece right of Line 1", or "the part" / "the offcut" once a one-sided chamfer names the side kept), with "Clamp both sides of the line (or use tape, vacuum or glue)". A setup without items names both sides. **Decided here:** a warning, not an error (the user may hold the offcut by hand-placed means DFYOM does not model).
  - Tests: `toolpath/line-through.test.ts` (open ends, alternation, `deepCuts` clean, heightmap slot covered and nothing beside it, arc leads), `design/line-through.test.ts`, `plan/line-through-plan.test.ts` (the user's 100 mm aluminium plate, Ø6.22 flat 22 mm from the front edge, 0.4 breakthrough: two pieces, no errors, `deepCuts` clean, golden `line-cut-off.nc`, virtual machine; the cut-off after a pocket; clamps on both sides silence the warning), `LineShape.test.tsx` (Through, axe), `e2e/line.e2e.ts` (Through → Plan).
- **SL4 (built):** the one-sided chamfer along a through Line.
  - **Model:** chamfer `edgeSide?: 'left' | 'right'` (DTO, `design.chamfer.add/update` arg); no `schemaVersion` change. Invariant `chamferSide`: a chamfer of a groove edge (below) needs `edgeSide`, any other edge refuses it; `loops` and `cornerLift` do not apply. SL4 itself took only a **through** Line; the follow-up below adds a Line with a depth and a profile `on`.
  - **Geometry:** a third chamfer geometry (`WallChamferGeometry`, `geometry/wall-chamfer.ts`): the Line's element, half the slot width and the kept side. The wall is the element offset by half the slot toward the kept side; at an open end it continues along the tangent to the stock face's edge, at an inner end it is trimmed by the cone's reach (round ends are not chamfered). The removal solid is a V-groove swept along that wall (`wall-chamfer-solids.ts`); DFM checks the library only; tool fit refuses a cone wide enough to reach the slot's other wall (rule `otherWall`).
  - **Plan / toolpath:** the edge dependency orders the chamfer after its Line (a freeing cut); the wall is offset by half of **the plan's tool on the Line** (`edgeSlot`), not the design's display cutter. The toolpath reuses the edge chamfer's rings by level (`edgeChamferPasses`, DF142: same V-bit fit and levels); each ring is a Line along the offset wall at its depth, cut in one pass with the through Line's side entry at open ends and ramp-back at inner ends (`toolpath/wall-chamfer.ts`). The cut-off warning names "the part" (the kept side) and "the offcut".
  - **UI:** Chamfer in the through Line's row menu (side Left by default); the inspector shows **Side that stays: Left / Right** (looking from the line's start to its end) and no Diameter, Side or Corner lift. **Decided here:** the viewport arrow and click-to-pick are a follow-up.
  - Tests: `toolpath/wall-chamfer.test.ts` (heightmap bevel on the kept wall, other wall untouched, gouge 0; levels each within its pass, `deepCuts` clean; wall ends; `otherWall`), `plan/line-chamfer-plan.test.ts` (the user's plate: cut-off then 0.5 × 45° on the left with the 1/4" 90° V-bit, order, no errors, warning names part and offcut, `deepCuts` clean after the slot, heightmap, golden `line-cut-off-chamfer.nc`, virtual machine; refusals), `Chamfer.test.tsx` (row menu, side picker, axe), `e2e/line.e2e.ts` (Through → Chamfer side → Plan).
- **SL4 follow-up (built):** the one-sided chamfer along a Line with a depth and along a profile `on`.
  - **Edges** (`chamferEdgeKind` 'wall', `isWallEdgeLine`): a through Line; a Line with a constant or start → end depth along one inline Straight / Arc; a profile `on` (open path or closed loop). **Decided here:** a Line in Shape mode or with levels stays refused ("not yet": many lines, varying width); `chamferEdge`'s message says so.
  - **Side of a closed loop:** `edgeSide` is taken along the loop counter-clockwise, so **left is inside, right outside** whatever the shape's drawn direction; the inspector names them **Inside / Outside** (Left / Right on an open path).
  - **Straight walls:** a Line with a depth that a one-sided chamfer bevels is `walled` (geometry flag): like a through Line its groove is cut by a flat end mill (the design shows a Ø6 flat when its tool is automatic or not flat; the plan's tool fit refuses another type, "a Line with a one-sided chamfer needs a flat end mill"; the auto choice takes end mills). A profile already needs a flat end mill; its groove is its slot width (the plan's tool may be narrower).
  - **Floor (rule `chamferBelowGroove`, at `width`):** the bevel's depth must not pass the groove's wall: a Line's shallowest depth, a blind profile's depth, a through profile's height above its tabs (closed loop with tabs); the toolpath refuses it too (rule `belowGroove`).
  - **Wall path** (`geometry/wall-chamfer.ts`, `ChamferWall { elements, closed?, halfWidth, side, capped?, floor? }`): the centre path offset toward the kept side with its corners: a corner whose outside is kept is rounded about the corner (as the groove's wall is), an inside corner is trimmed where the offsets meet (the bevel is mitred). A capped end (the groove's round end inside the stock) shortens the axis by as little as keeps the cone's disc out of the stock beyond the end: 0 when the end is square on the stock edge (the bevel runs to the edge, entered from outside as SL4's side entry), the cone's reach inside the stock (round ends are not chamfered). Every axis point must lie its distance from the whole centre path: a curve tighter than the offset or a path that comes back within its own groove is refused (rules `tooTight`, `selfNear`; the removal solid is then empty and the plan names the error). With that, the cone cuts nowhere deeper than the kept wall's bevel and reaches the other wall only past `d` + half the groove (the `otherWall` fit). A through Line keeps SL4's path byte for byte.
  - **Plan:** unchanged otherwise (edge dependency, rings by level, DF142 levels); `edgeSlot` (the plan's tool on the Line) applies to any Line edge.
  - Tests: `toolpath/wall-chamfer-groove.test.ts` (inner ends, square and slanted stock-edge ends, an arc either side, an open path with corners either side, a closed loop inside and outside: heightmap gouge / other side / short / nothing past a capped end, `deepCuts` clean per ring; refusals `belowGroove`, `tooTight`, `selfNear`), `plan/groove-chamfer-plan.test.ts` (a Line 3 mm deep across the user's plate bevelled on its left, golden `line-groove-chamfer.nc`; a profile `on` along a 40 × 30 rectangle bevelled outside, golden `profile-on-chamfer.nc`; both heightmap-checked, `deepCuts` clean, virtual machine; refusals `chamferBelowGroove` and Shape mode), `chamfer.test.ts` (a profile `on` takes a side), `Chamfer.test.tsx` (Inside / Outside, axe). SL4's goldens are unchanged.
