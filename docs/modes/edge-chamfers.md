# Edge chamfers: chamfers along any operation's edge

Status: accepted (2026-11-25; hardening [DF77](../plans/hardening.md#intake-user-feedback-goes-here), user TG #257 and TG #325 "can't wait for chamfers on all operations"; the user took every recommendation of [Q117–Q121](#questions-for-the-user), TG #331 "go"; E1, E2 and E3 built on the recommendations, [Implementation (E1)](#implementation-e1), [Implementation (E2)](#implementation-e2), [Implementation (E3)](#implementation-e3); E4a (several passes, corner lift, chosen loops) built provisionally, [Implementation (E4a)](#implementation-e4a); E4b (bottom edges from a flipped setup, Q119) built provisionally, [Implementation (E4b)](#implementation-e4b)) · Spike: [S7](../research/2026-11-25-spike-s7-edge-chamfers.md) · Extends: [design-v0.4.md § Chamfer operation](design-v0.4.md#chamfer-operation-v04-circular-edges) (circular edges, H-E), [plan-v0.4.md § Chamfer toolpath](plan-v0.4.md#chamfer-toolpath) · Also: [tearout research G8](../research/2026-11-24-hardwood-tearout.md) (contour deburr) · Base: [design-v0.3.md](design-v0.3.md) (shapes), [geometry.md § 2.5D evaluation](../architecture/geometry.md#25d-evaluation-v01), [shapes.md](../architecture/shapes.md) · Terms: [glossary.md](../glossary.md)

v0.4 chamfers only the **circular mouth** of a drilling, a circle pocket or an inside circle profile. This doc extends the same chamfer operation to the top edge of **any pocket or profile**: the outline of a part, the rim of a cavity or cutout, the mouth of a pocket of any shape, the islands in it. The chamfer is cut by a V-bit or chamfer mill that follows the edge. It is also the "contour deburr" pass that removes frayed top edges in wood (G8). Everything not said here stays as v0.4 has it: width as the size (D45), angle, the depth as an alternative input, the chamfer mill tool type, feeds by the effective diameter.

## Model

The chamfer operation of [design-v0.4.md](design-v0.4.md#chamfer-operation-v04-circular-edges), with one rule changed:

| Parameter | Change                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `edgeOf`  | Id of an earlier **drilling, pocket (any shape) or profile with side `outside` or `inside`** (through, blind or a step, DF53), on the same face. Refused: facings (no edge), `on` profiles (a groove has two edges; later), other chamfers. A **through Line** is an edge too, with `edgeSide` naming the wall that stays ([engraved-patterns.md § Implementation (SL3–SL4)](engraved-patterns.md#implementation-sl3sl4)) |
| `width`   | Unchanged: the bevel's width at the face, measured from the edge into the material, default 0.5 mm (inch 1/64″). For a non-circular edge, no diameter is derived (none exists); the inspector shows the depth                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `angle`   | Unchanged (60°–120°)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

- **Rules:** `chamferEdge` lists the new kinds; `chamferOn` and the reference behaviour (removing the edge fails with `design.referenced`; moving it takes the chamfer along) are unchanged. A `stepdown` (stepped chamfer, F7) stays circular-only: rule `chamferStepped` refuses it on another edge.
- **Circles stay as they are** (EC-G). A drilling, a circle pocket or an inside circle profile keeps v0.4's exact cone, plunge entry and grouping of equal chamfers. A rectangle pocket, a region or a rotated rectangle takes the general path. A circle drawn as a region of two arcs takes the general path too (same result, no plunge).
- **No stored change.** The operation's stored shape is the same, and `edgeOf` just accepts more kinds: no `schemaVersion` bump, nothing to migrate.

### Which edges

- **Top edges only** (EC-F). The edge sits where the operation's walls meet its `on` face: a pocket's rim, a cutout's mouth, a part's outline. Bottom edges of through cuts face the spoilboard and can only be reached from a flipped setup (Q119). Floor edges inside a pocket cannot be reached by a V-bit from above.
- **All loops** of the operation's shape (EC-B, Q118): a pocket's outer loop and its islands, every piece of a multi-piece region, the outline of an outside profile, the opening of an inside profile. A loop is all or nothing; choosing loops (or stretches of a loop) is later if asked.
- **Steps and blind profiles** (DF53, rabbets): the edge at the face, the same as a through profile. The step's inner floor edge is a bottom edge (out of scope).
- **Pockets on a floor** (`on` = another pocket's floor): the chamfer bevels the mouth on that floor, at the floor's Z. That is the v0.4 rule for drillings.

### Removal solid

Spike S7 ([§ The idea](../research/2026-11-25-spike-s7-edge-chamfers.md#the-idea)): the V-bit whose axis follows the toolpath region P has swept P ⊕ ρ(z) at height z. The solid is that sweep, so it is what the tool cuts:

- **Offset-loft** (the normal path): P offset by ρ(z) at a few levels, built from the analytic offset of P's elements (parallels, joint arcs, trims; an arc whose radius reaches 0 collapses into its neighbours' trim). It runs on vertically from the face to the overshoot, as the circular cone does, and 0.04 mm past the edge into the air below (no film, the C2 rule). An inside edge is one loft. An outside edge is the V-groove between the grown and the shrunk loft (one annular mesh), so the offcut beyond the slot stays.
- **Fallback (topology change):** each level is checked against the 2D port's offset (loop count, area within the chord band). Where the offset changes structure between dt/2 and dt/2 + width (a tooth narrower than 2 × width, a notch shorter than the width, air narrower than the tip), the solid is the union of per-element swept pieces built as labelled meshes, **unioned in the kernel** (a float32 round trip of a unioned mesh is not valid: [S7 failure 1](../research/2026-11-25-spike-s7-edge-chamfers.md#failure-modes-found)).
- **Surfaces:** one display-only surface `chamfer1.bevel` (not a face target and not an expression name, like `chamfer1.cone`).
- **Inside corners:** at a sharp concave material corner (a pocket corner of radius 0) the V-bit leaves a cone of uncut material, (4 − π)/12 · h · w² per 90° corner with a pointed bit. The solid shows it (EC-C). A corner radius ≥ dt/2, which a milled pocket always has, gives the exact constant-width bevel. Convex corners come out as sharp mitres.
- **Several pieces** (patterns P3, [patterns.md § Implementation (P3)](patterns.md#implementation-p3)): an inside edge of several pieces is lofted and checked per piece (copies share one loft), pieces whose bevels meet go into different meshes unioned in the kernel, and only a piece whose own offset changes structure takes the fallback.
- **Budget:** S7 measured 3–45 ms added to the evaluation for edges up to ≈ 250 elements (enclosure cavity + 14 ms; enclosure outline + 42 ms, ≈ + 20 ms expected with the annular mesh). The fallback costs ≈ 0.6 ms per element, more above ≈ 200 elements. The operation cache keeps an unchanged chamfer's solid.
- **Code:** `src/core/geometry/chamfer-solids.ts` (loft and fallback, next to `shape-solids.ts`), sharing the toolpath region with the generator through `outline.ts`.

## Toolpath (Plan)

A new generator `generateEdgeChamfer` (`src/core/toolpath/edge-chamfer.ts`), request kind `edgeChamfer`. Circles keep `generateChamfer`.

- **Path:** the edge loops offset into the air by dt/2 through the 2D port: a pocket's region shrunk, an outside profile's shape grown. The same region feeds the solid. Tip Z = face − width / tan(α / 2), so the cone reaches `width` into the material at the face, as the circular contour ring does (r = (d − dt) / 2).
- **Corners:** at a convex material corner the path rounds by dt/2 (a pointed bit turns in place), which leaves a sharp mitre. At a concave corner the path keeps the edge's radius minus dt/2. A sharp inside corner keeps its cone; the V-carving corner move is later (Q121).
- **Moves:** per loop, rapid to the start point at clearance and retract, feed down at `plungeRate` to the tip Z **in the air** (the path lies dt/2 off the wall, and the edge's operation cut that air earlier), one full loop at `feedRate`, rapid out. Each loop starts at the middle of its longest line, else its longest arc: the pocket finishing pass's rule ([plan-v0.3.md § Implementation (T1)](plan-v0.3.md#implementation-t1-region-level-generator)), which keeps the plunge off corners. Climb: clockwise round an outside edge, counter-clockwise inside a pocket or opening, the same as profiles. Loops in nearest-neighbour order. One pass of kind `finish`. Arcs are written as `G2`/`G3` ([plan-v0.3.md T-B](plan-v0.3.md#decisions)).
- **Strategy:** `direction` (climb / conventional), as profiles; no `entry` (only the loop entry exists; an explicit `plunge` is refused, `strategy.entry`). A lead-in arc is later.
- **Order:** after the edge's operation (the existing reference rule, `plan.order` blocks the reverse). An outline's chamfer therefore runs after the through profile, while the part hangs on its tabs: the V-bit's cut is light and the tabs take it. Several chamfers with the same tool form one tool block.
- **Feeds:** [F10](../architecture/feeds-speeds.md#chamfer-mills-v04): effective diameter De = dt + 2 × width, engagement `chamfer { depth }`.
- **Checks:** angle (0.5°), reach to the tip Z, the slant ([Tool](#tool)). The tip check becomes a DFM finding for non-circular edges (`dfm.chamferIncomplete`), because a tip too wide for a narrow neck still cuts the rest of the edge.

## Tool

The `chamferMill` type (V-bits included) as in v0.4. Fit (`chamferFit`): angle within 0.5° and a cutting edge covering the bevel, (DC − dt) / 2 ≥ width (rule `slant`; the v0.4 rule with D − d = 2 × width). The tip rule (`tip` < edge diameter) applies to circles only. Auto choice: library tools by the usual ranking, else a generalized chamfer mill of the chamfer's angle (`CHAMFER_MILL_STANDARD`). A bevel wider than every tool's cutting edge gets `dfm.chamferNoTool` (rule `slant`) and needs a larger chamfer mill. Several passes are later (Q120).

## UI

- **Row menu** ([ui.md § Features (v0.4)](../conventions/ui.md#features-v04), row menu): **Chamfer** on every pocket row and every `outside` / `inside` profile row, owned rows included (a hole's counterbore, as the owned drilling today). It sends `design.chamfer.add` with that operation as its edge and the defaults, then selects the chamfer. `on` profiles and facings do not offer it.
- **Chamfer inspector:** **Edge** `Select` lists every eligible operation ("Pocket 2 · cavity", "Profile 1 · outline"). Pick mode highlights the edge loops in the 3D view as amber lines on the face. Then Width, Depth (alternative input) and Angle as today. The section illustration draws a straight wall with its bevel for a non-circular edge (no diameter), and the hole section for circles.
- **Edge operation's inspector:** a read-only line "Chamfered by Chamfer 1 · 0.5 × 45°" that selects the chamfer, so the edge break is visible where the edge is edited.
- **Features:** a hole feature's owned pocket or drilling row offers Chamfer as above. Chamfer on the source operation of a Clones adds the chamfer to the Clones' source in the same command (one undo step), so every instance is chamfered; instances stay read-only.
- **Plan:** the chamfer is a plan operation like any other (navigator row, tool, feeds, preview). The plan inspector shows the tip Z, the loops and the finding markers.

## Agent API

No new tool. `design.chamfer.add { partId, edgeOf, width?, depth?, angle?, name? }` and `design.chamfer.update` keep their arguments. `edgeOf`'s description widens to "Earlier drilling, pocket or outside/inside profile". The `chamferEdge` refusal names the accepted kinds and, for an `on` profile or a facing, says why. `design.operation.list` and `plan.operation.list` already show chamfers. `tools/list` measured **107.4 K** (107,440 chars, 139 tools; `design_chamfer_add` 563 chars, `design_chamfer_update` 648) on 2026-11-25; the change adds ≈ 0.05 K, well inside the 120 K cap.

## Implementation (E1)

Built 2026-11-25 on a branch, **provisionally**: on the recommended answers to Q117–Q121 (own operation from the row menu, all loops, top edges only, one pass, the inside-corner cone left). A different answer changes the UI entry (Q117), a loop filter in `loopChamferAir` (Q118) or the generator's pass loop (Q120); the model, solid and plan wiring stay.

- **Geometry input.** One `chamfer` kind with a sub-variant: `LoopChamferGeometry` (`src/core/geometry/model.ts`) carries `edge { shape, side: 'inside', depth? }` (the edge operation's placed shape and depth) instead of `center` / `edgeDiameter` / `diameter`; `isLoopChamfer` tells them apart. A circular edge builds exactly the v0.4 input (byte-equal goldens, EC-G). `loopChamferDepth` = width / tan(α/2). The schema is `z.union([discriminatedUnion(…), LoopChamferGeometrySchema])` (zod cannot hold two `chamfer` options in one discriminated union). `chamferEdgeKind` (`design/operation.ts`) says `circle`, `loop` or no edge; the design model is unchanged.
- **Air region.** `loopChamferAir` (`geometry/outline.ts`): the edge shape's region, the same for the solid and the toolpath.
- **Removal solid** (`geometry/chamfer-solids.ts`): S7's offset-loft for a **pointed** V-bit (the design does not know the tool; a tip only matters in E3's narrow necks and in sharp inside corners), the 0.04 mm run (`LOOP_CHAMFER_RUN`), levels at most 0.5 mm of offset apart, each checked against the 2D port's offset (loop count, area within the chord band). Fallback: per chord of the flattened loop a stadium frustum, each its own removal mesh, unioned by the evaluator in the kernel. Display surface `<id>.bevel`. `loopChamferConstruction` reports `loft` / `fallback` (tests).
- **Rules.** `chamferEdge` accepts any pocket and inside profile; the message names the kinds and says why a facing, a chamfer, an `on` profile or (until E2) an outside profile is refused. `chamferStepped` refuses a `stepdown` on a loop edge.
- **DFM.** `dfm.chamferTooDeep` generalized (pocket depth, blind inside profile depth); the B4 through-floor check stays circular. `dfm.chamferNoTool` for a loop: no library mill of the angle whose cutting edge covers the width (params `angle`, `width`). `chamferMeets` / `chamferIncomplete` are E3.
- **Tool fit** (`geometry/tool-fit.ts`): the loop form of `chamferFit` checks type, angle and the slant (`coneSlant(dt, DC) ≥ coneSlant(0, 2w)`); no tip rule (EC-E). The auto choice is unchanged (library, else the generalized chamfer mill).
- **Plan** (`plan/strategy.ts`, `checks.ts`): entry is always the loop entry (`contour`); an explicit `plunge` is refused (rule `loopEntry`, `strategy.entry`); `cutDepth` = the bevel depth (F10 feeds, reach). Loop chamfers are never grouped. Climb only from the plan (the generator supports conventional), as v0.4 rings.
- **Toolpath** (`toolpath/edge-chamfer.ts`, request kind `edgeChamfer`, `toolpath/inputs.ts`): the air shrunk by dt/2 through the 2D port, one ring per loop at tip Z, plunge in the air at `plungeRate`, start at the middle of the longest line, nearest-neighbour loop order, one `finish` pass. Refusals: tool type, angle, `slant`, and `tip` when no ring is left.
- **Other consumers.** `placed` bounds (shape grown by the width), clone footprints (`shapePart` grown by the width), flat patches (the air grown by the width), measure (no hole centre), split findings (the edge's own finding covers it).
- **UI.** Chamfer in the row menu of every pocket and inside profile row (owned rows included), the Edge `Select` lists them by name; no Diameter field and no section illustration for a loop edge yet (the wall section is E3); the viewport outline of a loop chamfer is its edge loops.
- **Agent API.** `edgeOf`: "Earlier drilling, pocket or inside profile". `tools/list` **107,412** chars (139 tools; was 107,440).
- **Tests.** `geometry/chamfer-solids.test.ts` (rounded rectangle and analytic rectangle against h(P·w/2 + πw²/3), sharp corners with the cone, an island, 60° on a floor, the fallback against the 2D oracle, the enclosure cavity rim against the oracle within half the chord band, `chamferTooDeep`); `toolpath/edge-chamfer.test.ts` (heightmap against the bevel, rules, refusals, request round trip, golden `edge-chamfer-island.txt`); `plan/edge-chamfer-toolpaths.test.ts` (tool choice, strategy, `loopEntry`, `slant`; the exit flow: a pocket with an island chamfered, generated, exported for GRBL and streamed through the virtual machine); `Chamfer.test.tsx` (row menu on a rectangle pocket).
- **Known gaps.** Split parts copy a loop chamfer with its edge into each piece: a pocket clipped by a split line gains that line as an edge, and the chamfer bevels it (fixed in E2, `sourceEdge`). No hidden e2e step yet (E2's exit).

## Implementation (E2)

Built 2026-11-25 on a branch on top of E1, **provisionally** on the same recommended answers.

- **Rules.** `chamferEdgeKind` says `loop` for any pocket and any profile whose side is not `on`; `chamferEdge` no longer refuses outside profiles. `edgeOf`: "Earlier drilling, pocket or outside/inside profile". `tools/list` **107,428** chars (139 tools; +16).
- **Geometry input.** `LoopChamferGeometry.edge.side` is `inside` or `outside`, plus an optional `within` (below). `loopChamferRegion` (was `loopChamferAir`, `geometry/outline.ts`) is the shape region for an inside edge and its outer loops (the part) for an outside one; `loopChamferAxis` moves it into the air by d (shrunk inside, grown outside), the one place the solid and the toolpath take their path from.
- **Face Z.** A loop chamfer on a stock face sits on the **material top at the edge**: the deepest floor of the earlier facings of that face (`materialTopZ`, `design/geometry-input.ts`). The enclosure's outline is profiled from `stock.top`, which its top facing takes 9.738 mm down; the bevel is on the faced top, not in the air. This applies to E1's inside profiles too; circular chamfers are unchanged (byte-equal goldens).
- **Removal solid.** An outside edge is one annular mesh: the grown rings (the air side) with the shrunk rings reversed (the part side), each side its own offset plan and port check; the V-groove of the spike, so the offcut beyond the slot is not touched. The swept-chords fallback covers either side's topology change (a thin part).
- **Toolpath.** The part grown by dt/2; the rings reversed so climb keeps the air on the left: **clockwise round the part** (G2 on the corners), counter-clockwise for conventional. Golden `edge-chamfer-outside.txt`; `edge-chamfer-island.txt` unchanged.
- **Order and tabs.** The chamfer runs after its profile (the E1 order rule), with the part held by the profile's tabs; it does not skip tabs. At the tab's top the cone is in the air unless the tip Z is below the tab top (thin stock or a wide bevel), where it trims the tab's top corner, which the 3D view shows. Chamfering before the profile (in the solid stock) is not offered: the path's plunge needs the slot's air.
- **Split pieces** (fixes E1's known gap). A piece's copy of a loop chamfer gets a piece-only `sourceEdge { shape, within }` (`ChamferDto`, written only by `design.split.set`; optional, no schema bump): the **source's unclipped** edge shape in the piece frame, and the piece's cell in the face frame. The bevel follows the source's real edges; the solid is masked to the cell grown by the reach at the face (`cut` mask) and the toolpath clips each ring to that box into **open runs** (`clipRing`). The split line, keys and sockets are never chamfered. A chamfer whose edge is the source outline points at the piece outline (`<split>_outline`); chamfers at a piece's own stock face (fromFace on the up face) are now kept in pieces too (they were dropped).
- **Other consumers.** Flat patches: an outside chamfer removes the band offset(−w) − offset(w) at its face; bounds, clones and DFM take the E1 paths (nothing outside-specific).
- **UI.** Chamfer in the row menu of every outside profile row (the existing `isChamferEdge` gate); `noEdge` / `edgeMissing` texts name profiles.
- **Tests.** `chamfer-solids.test.ts` (an outside rectangle analytically, a rounded R8 outline at 60° against M − M⊖ρ, the thin-part fallback, the `within` mask, the enclosure outline within the chord band); `edge-chamfer.test.ts` (heightmap against the bevel round a part, climb clockwise, open runs on a piece, golden); `edge-chamfer-toolpaths.test.ts` (**E2 exit headless**: the enclosure outline 1.5 × 45° after its profile, bevel tip at −9.738 − 1.5, both setups exported and streamed through the virtual machine; a two-piece split by x = 125 whose piece chamfer follows `split1_outline`'s source edge and never the split line); `chamfer.test.ts` (outside accepted, `on` refused).
- **Known gaps.** A split piece's chamfer zRange (findings) uses the `on` face, not the faced top (fixed in E3). A male key within the reach (dt/2 + w) of an outline corner can be nicked by the bevel run past the cell (a finding in E3). The Clones source rule (a clone of a chamfered outline) is not addressed. The user's real cut is open.

## Implementation (E3)

Built 2026-11-25 on a branch on top of E2, **provisionally** on the same recommended answers.

- **Findings** (`geometry/chamfer-findings.ts`, called from `dfm.ts` `chamfer` for loop chamfers when the 2D port is there; results cached per port and edge). Both in the face frame, `at` = the main marker and `points` = up to 10 markers (flat u, v pairs, as `dfm.restMaterial`); sharp inside corners are left out by the rest-material corner zones (`dfm.cornerSharp` covers them); on a split piece only within its cell.
  - `dfm.chamferMeets` (parameter `width`, warning): the material within 2w + 1 mm of the edge (a band from the 2D port: the air grown minus the air, or the part minus the part shrunk) has rest material for a disc of radius w (`findRestMaterial`), so a tooth, an island or a wall between two of the chamfer's loops is narrower than 2w. The wall's width by bisecting that radius (0.01 mm); the suggestion is at most half of it. Params `wall`, `width`. Only the chamfer's own loops: a wall between two chamfers is not checked yet.
  - `dfm.chamferIncomplete` (parameter `tool`, warning, so it shows at the tool field): only for the operation's own chamfer mill with a tip (`tipDiameter` > 0; the generalized one is pointed). Gated by the air's rest material for the tip radius; then the edge is sampled every 0.5 mm and a sample farther than dt/2 + 0.01 from the tip's axis region (the air shrunk by dt/2) is not chamfered fully (narrower than asked, or not at all). Params `length` (the edge not reached), `tipDiameter`, `tool`, `edgeOf`. The generator still refuses only when the tip fits nowhere (`tip`).
  - Measured: spike S7's comb pocket (teeth 6 / 2 / 1 mm at w 1.5: none / wall 2 / wall 1, eight markers), the dumbbell (0.4 mm neck, tip 0.6: ≈ 40 mm of edge, none for a pointed tip or a 0.3 tip), an island Ø 2, an outline tongue 2 mm wide and a 0.4 mm notch; the enclosure's outline and cavity at 1.5 with a 0.2 tip give none in ≈ 24 ms uncached.
- **Bevels wider than the cutting edge (Q120).** Already one pass with a finding since E1: `dfm.chamferNoTool` (library, rule `slant`), `dfm.toolUnfit` rule `slant` for the operation's own tool, and the generator's `slant` refusal. No change.
- **Split pieces** (E2's known gaps). The `cutsFeature` `cone` finding checks a loop chamfer's real bevel, from the material top at its edge (`onZ`, the faced top) rather than its `on` face (`zRange(…, bevel)`, `split/pieces.ts`); which piece keeps the chamfer is unchanged. On the enclosure (bevel −9.738 … −11.238) a level at −1 no longer reports it and a level at −10.5 does. A new `cutsFeature` reason **`chamferKey`** (`split/findings.ts` `bevelNicksKey`): a loop chamfer on the source outline whose bevel (width w into the material) comes within the male piece's run past the split line (dt/2 + w) of a key's knob, sampled on the knob's box grown by w. Keys keep a wall of one outline tool Ø to the outline, so it fires only for bevels wider than that.
- **UI.** The chamfer inspector draws the **wall section** for a general edge (`wallChamferSection`, `viewport/chamfer-section.ts`; values from `wallSectionValues`, `chamfer-view.ts`): one wall with its bevel, air left and material right, parts `width`, `depth`, `angle`, `edgeOf` (no `diameter`), a bevel reaching a blind floor cut there, a deep or through wall fading out as the hole section; label "Section: chamfer 0.5 mm wide at 90°, 0.5 mm deep, along the wall of Pocket 1, 4 mm deep.". The Edge options name a general edge with its role: "Pocket 2 · rim", "Profile 1 · opening", "Profile 1 · outline". Diameter stays hidden for a general edge (E1). Messages for both findings and `chamferKey` in `messages/dfm.ts`.
- **Not done** (E3 rows of [Slices](#slices) left for later): the "Chamfered by" line in the edge operation's inspector, pick mode drawing the edge loops (it still tints the operations' surfaces), the finding markers drawn in the viewport (they are in `params` as for `dfm.restMaterial`), meets between two chamfers, the L-review.
- **Tests.** `geometry/chamfer-findings.test.ts` (the cases above and the wiring in `parameterChecks` with an own tool); `viewport/chamfer-section.test.ts` (the wall section); `Chamfer.test.tsx` (the rectangle pocket's edge option, wall section, no diameter part, axe); `split/keys.test.ts` (`bevelNicksKey`, the panel keyed with a chamfered outline: no finding); `plan/edge-chamfer-toolpaths.test.ts` (the split level against the faced top). Goldens unchanged.

## Implementation (E4a)

Planned and built 2026-11-25 on a branch on top of E3, **provisional** until the user picks E4 or split parts 4d next (recommendation: E4). It takes the "later" halves of Q118, Q120 and Q121 as options; the event-split loft and bottom edges (Q119) stay in E4. Nothing stored changes for a chamfer that does not use them: no `schemaVersion` bump, byte-equal goldens.

- **Several passes for a wide bevel (Q120 option 2).** When the bevel is wider than the tool's cutting edge, c = (DC − dt) / 2 < width, the generator cuts it in **levels**, stepping down and out; a bevel within one cutting edge keeps the E1 ring exactly. With t = tan(α/2), h = width / t, rings spaced at most c apart (cusp q = max(0, c − dt) / (2t) between them) and a level step Δ = c / t − q, there are n = ⌈h / Δ⌉ levels at tip depths z_k = k·h / n. Each level runs rings from the edge's ring (dt/2 into the air, its plunge in the air) out into the material in equal steps ≤ c to the **bevel ring**, whose cone touches the bevel (axis width − z_k·t − dt/2 into the material); the last level is the E1 ring. Inner rings are `rough` passes, bevel rings `finish`. Why it is safe: the tool's body above its cutting edge (Ø DC) only meets depths the previous level cleared (Δ + q ≤ c / t), and every ring's cut lies inside the final bevel (an offset by m swept by a cone of radius ρ stays within m + ρ of the edge, which the bevel ring makes equal to the bevel), so **the removal solid is unchanged**. A ring after the first of a level plunged at most Δ + q into material, within the cutting edge (E4b steps in from the air ring instead). No max stepdown argument yet (the chamfer's feeds have no stepdown field); the feeds keep the full bevel depth as engagement (conservative). Rings run in the edge offset's loops (the 2D port, a negative offset grows into the material), nearest-neighbour within a level.
- **Findings for wide bevels.** The loop form of `chamferFit` drops the slant rule: any chamfer mill of the angle with a cutting edge (DC > dt) fits, so `dfm.chamferNoTool` (rule `slant`), `dfm.toolUnfit` (rule `slant`) and the generator's `slant` refusal no longer fire for a general edge; the plan's preview shows the passes. Circles kept the v0.4 one-ring rule until [DF142](plan-v0.4.md#chamfers-wider-than-the-cutting-edge-hardening-df142), which cuts them in the same levels. The auto choice is unchanged (largest size class first), so a library tool that covers the bevel in one pass still wins over a smaller one of the same angle; since DF142 one pass ranks before levels explicitly, and levels need a shank no wider than DC (rule `shank`), which this argument assumes.
- **Corner lift (Q121 option 2).** Per chamfer opt-in, `cornerLift: true` (absent = off). At a **sharp concave material corner between two lines** of a chosen loop (a convex corner of the air, turn at most 170° so the mitre stays within 11.5 × the width), the bevel rings lift the V-bit along the corner's bisector so the two bevels meet in a crisp mitre (the V-carving corner move): from the ring's point on the bisector, along it at the tip Z to the crease (multi-pass levels only), up the crease to the face at V + b·(width − dt/2) / sin(θ/2) (V the corner, b the bisector into the material, θ the corner's angle), and back the same way, at the feed rate. The **solid models it**: per lifted corner a kite (the edge corner, the two bevels' ends and their mitre point) lofted at the loft's levels, its own removal mesh unioned in the kernel; at a lifted corner the solid is then the mitre instead of the cone of E1. Rounded corners (radius < dt/2, where the tip cannot follow either) and corners with an arc keep the E1 behaviour (later). A circular edge refuses it (rule `chamferLift`).
- **Choosing loops (Q118 option 2).** `loops: number[]` on the chamfer (absent = every loop): indices into the edge's loops in region order, per piece its outer loop then its islands (0 = the outer loop of the first piece; an outside edge has one loop). Rule `chamferLoops`: a circular edge refuses it, an index must exist, at least one (an empty list is refused by the args); duplicates are dropped and the indices sorted. The **toolpath** offsets the whole edge as before (so an unchosen island still keeps the tool out) and keeps each ring's stretches whose nearest edge loop is chosen (open runs where a ring borders a chosen and an unchosen loop, as split pieces). The **solid** of a chosen subset is the V-groove band of each chosen loop (the E2 outside construction per loop: grown ring and shrunk ring, the swept-chords fallback per loop), so an unchosen loop keeps its sharp edge; all loops chosen is the E1/E2 solid. Flat patches take the union of the chosen loops' bands; `dfm.chamferIncomplete` samples the chosen loops only, `dfm.chamferMeets` keeps the pieces within 2 × width + 1 mm of a chosen loop (a wall between a chosen and an unchosen loop still reports, a known gap). Lift corners come from the chosen loops only.
- **Model and API.** `Chamfer.loops: Option<number[]>`, `Chamfer.cornerLift: boolean`; DTO `loops?` and `cornerLift?: true` (left out when off, so records stay byte-identical). `design.chamfer.add` / `.update` take `loops` (array, `null` = every loop on update) and `cornerLift` (boolean). The geometry input carries `edge.loops?` and `cornerLift?: true`. Split pieces copy both (the indices refer to `sourceEdge`'s unclipped shape).
- **UI** ([ui.md § Chamfer inspector](../conventions/ui.md#chamfer-inspector)). For a general edge, a **Loops** group of checkboxes when the edge has more than one loop ("Rim", "Island 1", …; for several pieces "Outline 2" …), all checked by default; unchecking the last one is not offered; checking all sends `loops: null`. A **Corner lift** switch with the note "Lift the V-bit into sharp inside corners for a crisp mitre." The plan's preview shows the levels and the lift moves.
- **Agent API.** `tools/list` measured in [llm-api.md](../architecture/llm-api.md) (E4a line). `plan.design.operation.update` did not take `loops` or `cornerLift` (fixed in [E4b](#implementation-e4b)).
- **Known gaps.** The Loops group lists loops only with the 2D port loaded (a named shape); placed bounds ignore a lifted mitre's overshoot; flat patches draw the cone, not the mitre.
- **Tests.** `toolpath/edge-chamfer.test.ts` (level count and depths for a bevel wider than the cutting edge, every cut inside the bevel by heightmap against the single-pass bevel, the shank never below the previous level, lift moves at a rectangle's sharp corners and none at rounded ones, chosen loops; golden `edge-chamfer-passes.txt`, a 3 mm bevel with a Ø 4 mm pointed 90° V-bit in three levels, corner lift and the outer loop of a sharp pocket with an island); `geometry/chamfer-solids.test.ts` (the kite's volume at a 90° corner, (4 − π)/12·h·w² added to E1's; the outer loop only leaves the island's edge sharp); `design/chamfer.test.ts` (args, rules `chamferLoops` and `chamferLift`, round trip); `plan/edge-chamfer-toolpaths.test.ts` (a 3.5 bevel now chooses the Ø 6 mm mill and cuts rough and finish levels to −3.5; loops and lift through the plan); `Chamfer.test.tsx` (Loops and Corner lift). Existing goldens byte-equal.

## Implementation (E4b)

Planned and built 2026-11-25 on a branch on top of E4a, **provisional** like E4a (Q119 taken as option (2) of a later slice: a chamfer on the opposite face). It also closes two E4a gaps: the step-in after a level's first ring, and `loops` / `cornerLift` on `plan.design.operation.update`. Nothing stored changes for a top chamfer: no new field, no `schemaVersion` bump.

- **Model.** A **bottom chamfer** is a chamfer whose `on` is the stock face opposite its edge's face (`on: 'stock.bottom'` for a profile on `stock.top`, `stock.side[2]` for one on `stock.side[0]`, …). Only a **through, non-circular outside or inside profile** has a bottom edge (`bottomEdgeFace`): a pocket has a floor (except a pattern's **through pocket** on a stock face, which has one since [patterns P5](patterns.md#implementation-p5)), a blind profile a step, and a circular through edge (drilling, circle profile) keeps the v0.4 circle path, so none of them gets one yet. Rule `chamferOn` allows the edge's face or, for such a profile, the opposite face; any other face is refused and the message names the faces allowed. `design.chamfer.add` / `.update` take `on` (default: the edge's face; on update a bottom chamfer stays on the bottom unless `on` says otherwise). When the profile becomes blind or moves to another face, `followEdges` moves the chamfer to the edge's face (a bottom chamfer of a profile still through stays on the opposite face).
- **Geometry.** The edge's shape is mirrored into the chamfer's face frame (`faceToFace`: top → bottom maps (u, v) to (u, W − v); side faces by their own frames), the face Z is the opposite face's faced top (`materialTopZ`, so a faced bottom puts the bevel on the faced surface), and the bevel is cut from that face downwards as on the top. The profile is through, so the air beside the edge is the same slot from both sides; `chamferTooDeep` never fires.
- **Tabs.** A through profile with tabs leaves the part in the stock: the bottom setup comes after the top setup's cutout with the part held by its tabs (the existing `plan.cutoutBeforeSetup` warning says so). The bottom chamfer **lifts over each tab** instead of cutting it: the geometry input carries `edge.tabs`, the profile's resolved tab centres as points on the edge in the chamfer's frame with the tab width, and the toolpath leaves out every stretch of a ring whose nearest edge point lies within the **tab gap** g = tab width / 2 + width + dt / 2 + 1 mm of a tab centre (open runs between tabs, bisected to 0.25 mm). The solid masks a disc of radius g(dt = 0) − width / 2 round each tab, so the 3D view keeps the edge sharp there (an approximation: the real bevel ends in the cone's taper). New finding **`dfm.chamferTabs`** (info, parameter `edgeOf`, params `count`, `gap`, `edgeOf`, marker points at the tabs): "The bottom bevel stops 5 mm short across each of the 4 tabs of Profile 1, so they keep holding the part. Finish those stretches by hand when you remove the tabs." Top chamfers ignore tabs (they never reach the tab's height).
- **Setups.** `deriveSetups` puts an operation whose edge (or `on` surface) is made in a setup of another direction (`crossSetupDependencies`) into a setup of its own direction **after** that dependency's setup: a board gives Top, Bottom; the enclosure (bottom facing first) gives Bottom, Top, Bottom 2. A rebuild places a new operation in the last setup of its direction at or after its dependencies' setups, else in a new one after them.
- **Step-in (E4a gap).** A level's inner ring used to plunge up to Δ + q into material. Now every ring inside the material (offset < dt/2) is entered by plunging at the nearest point of its level's **air ring** (offset dt/2, already cleared) and feeding across at depth to the ring's start; each open run retracts straight up at its end. One-ring chamfers are unchanged. This changes the golden `edge-chamfer-passes.txt` (the only one with levels): inner rings now plunge at the air ring and step 2 mm across (cut 770.93 → 773.93 mm, rapids 105 → 102 mm, 68.11 → 68.30 s); the other goldens are byte-equal.
- **Plan copies (E4a gap).** `plan.design.operation.update` takes `loops` and `cornerLift` like `design.chamfer.update`.
- **Split pieces.** A piece keeps a bottom chamfer of its source outline on its own opposite face; `sourceEdge.shape` is mirrored like the edge, and the clip (`within`) is already in the chamfer's frame, so only the source outline is chamfered, never the split line.
- **UI** ([ui.md § Chamfer inspector](../conventions/ui.md#chamfer-inspector)). For a through outside or inside profile, a **Side** segmented control under Edge: "Top edge" / "Bottom edge" (sends `on`), with the note "The bottom edge is cut from a flipped setup; the bevel stops at the tabs."
- **Agent API.** No new tool: `on` on `design.chamfer.add` / `.update` and `loops` / `cornerLift` on `plan.design.operation.update`; `tools/list` measured in [llm-api.md](../architecture/llm-api.md) (E4b line).
- **Known gaps.** Circular through edges (drillings, circle profiles) have no bottom chamfer; flat patches and placed bounds ignore the tab gaps; the flip's alignment (DF78) is not checked against the chamfer width by any finding; when a through profile moves to the opposite face, the bottom chamfer follows the edge's face (it becomes a top chamfer).
- **Tests.** `toolpath/edge-chamfer.test.ts` (every plunge of a levelled chamfer lies in the air at least dt/2 from the edge; tabs give open runs, no cut within the gap); `geometry/chamfer-solids.test.ts` (tab masks remove less volume); `design/chamfer.test.ts` (the mirror and tab points, `dfm.chamferTabs`, refusals for blind profiles, pockets, drillings and side faces, `followEdges`); `plan/edge-chamfer-toolpaths.test.ts` (**E4b exit**: a 100 × 60 × 12 board outlined with 4 tabs and chamfered 1 × 45° top and bottom plans Top then Bottom, no errors, cuts to Z −1 on the bottom face with no cut near the tabs, exports and streams every setup through the virtual machine; a rebuild puts a new bottom chamfer in Bottom 2; `plan.design.operation.update` with `loops` / `cornerLift`; a split piece's bottom chamfer stays off the split line); `Chamfer.test.tsx` (Side).

## Decided here (not questions)

- **Default width** stays 0.5 mm: the G8 deburr size, and the v0.4 default.
- **Order:** after the edge's own operation, never before. No separate "chamfer last" rule: a chamfer is a finishing cut on an edge that already exists.
- **One pass, `finish`** kind: one ring covers the bevel (EC-E). Several rings would be clearing a wedge, as v0.4 concluded.
- **No automatic chamfers.** Wood parts get no edge break unless the user adds one. A plan hint for wood ("add a 0.5 mm chamfer against fuzz") can come later with the tearout work.
- **Circles unchanged** (EC-G): the general path would give the same cut, but v0.4's plunge entry and grouping of equal chamfers are better for holes.

## Questions for the user

Numbering continues after [split-parts.md](split-parts.md#questions-for-the-user)'s Q116. "Q117 ok" takes the recommendation.

1. **Q117 – How you add a chamfer to an edge.** (1) A chamfer operation of its own, added from the pocket's or profile's row menu (right-click → Chamfer), as holes get one today; it has its own row, tool and place in the plan. (2) A "Chamfer top edge" width field in the pocket's and profile's own inspector, no separate row. (3) Both: the field creates and edits the operation of (1). _Recommendation: (1)._ It is how v0.4 already works for holes. A chamfer needs its own tool, order and feeds in the plan anyway, and the edge's inspector shows "Chamfered by Chamfer 1" so the link is visible.
2. **Q118 – Pockets with islands and shapes with several loops.** (1) The chamfer runs round every loop of the operation (rim and islands). (2) You choose loops in the chamfer's inspector. (3) The outer loop only. _Recommendation: (1) now, (2) later if you miss it._ Deburring usually wants every edge, and choosing loops needs a loop picker that the first slices can do without.
3. **Q119 – Bottom edges.** The underside edges of a through outline or cutout can only be chamfered from a flipped setup. (1) Later, as its own slice when you ask. (2) In the first slices, as a chamfer on the opposite face. (3) Never: sand them by hand. _Recommendation: (1)._ Most parts need only the visible top edge. A bottom chamfer depends on the flip alignment (DF78) being accurate to well under the chamfer width.
4. **Q120 – Bevels wider than one V-bit pass** (a large decorative bevel round the enclosure, wider than the bit's cutting edge). (1) One pass only for now: a wider bevel asks for a larger chamfer mill (finding), and several passes come in a later slice. (2) Several stepped passes in the first version. _Recommendation: (1)._ Edge breaks and deburring (0.3–2 mm) are the common case and fit one pass of a 90° Ø12 bit (up to ≈ 6 mm). Several passes are a clearing strategy for the wedge, with their own toolpath and finish questions.
5. **Q121 – Sharp inside corners.** A V-bit following an edge cannot reach into a sharp inside corner (a pocket corner drawn with radius 0). It leaves a small cone, about 0.07 mm³ per 90° corner at 1 × 45°. (1) Leave it; the 3D view shows it, and real pocket corners are rounded by the end mill anyway. (2) Optionally lift the V-bit along the corner's bisector (the V-carving corner move) for a crisp mitre. (3) Always do (2). _Recommendation: (1) now, (2) as a later option._ Milled pockets have corner radii ≥ the end mill's radius, where the chamfer comes out exact, and sharp corners already get `dfm.cornerSharp`.
