# Split parts: one part cut from several stocks

Status: accepted (proposal task 1.8, written 2026-11-24 after the user asked again, TG #311; the user took every recommendation of [Q109–Q116](#questions-for-the-user), TG #317 "go") · Proposal: [plans/proposal-multi-setup-alignment.md § 5](../plans/proposal-multi-setup-alignment.md#5-phase-4--split-parts-design) (S1–S4, [Q68](../plans/proposal-multi-setup-alignment.md#questions-for-the-user)) · Research: [research § D](../research/2026-11-23-multi-setup-alignment.md#d-splitting-one-part-across-several-stocks) · Base: [design.md](design.md), [features.md § Expansion and owned operations](features.md#expansion-and-owned-operations), [design-v0.3.md](design-v0.3.md) (shapes), [geometry.md § 2.5D evaluation](../architecture/geometry.md#25d-evaluation-v01), [alignment.md § Alignment aids](alignment.md#alignment-aids) (registration holes) · Terms: [glossary.md](../glossary.md)

The user (2026-11-24, TG #311): "any progress on splitting a large part into separate parts? I was trying to do it for temp/enclosure_1x.dfyom but haven't found an option." There is none yet: phase 4 of the proposal was not scheduled. This doc specifies it so it can be accepted and built in slices, smallest first ([§ Slices](#slices)).

**In one paragraph.** The user designs the part whole. A **split** on that part names straight **split lines** across its top (vertical cuts) and **split levels** (horizontal cuts at a Z). The cells between them are **pieces**. Each piece becomes a **derived part**: its own blank (piece bounds plus a margin per side), the source's operations mapped into it, and generated operations that cut it free along its outline and make the joint. Pieces stay in sync with the source; they are planned, exported and run like any part. Joints, DFM near the cuts and nesting onto library stock come in later slices.

## User story: the enclosure

From the user's project (`temp/enclosure_1x.dfyom`, read 2026-11-24; not copied into the repo). Part "enclosure 1": an open box at full scale (the reference STL at scale 1), faced 9.738 mm on both sides of an 80 mm Eucalyptus block, a cavity pocket 56.29 mm deep, four Ø4.06 and two Ø3.25 rim holes.

| Quantity                        | Enclosure                                     | Limit                                                                                     | Fits?                       |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Footprint                       | ≈ 249.5 × 177.0 mm (walls ≈ 7 mm, back 11 mm) | Largest library hardwood: Block 200 × 100.1 × 40 (Eucalipto)                              | No: longer and wider        |
| Height                          | 60.5 mm (floor ≈ 4.2 mm)                      | Library thickness 40 mm                                                                   | No                          |
| Cavity depth from the block top | 66 mm (9.7 facing + 56.3)                     | Longest end mill "extralong 1/4″": 50 mm flute, 62 mm stickout; the others ≤ 22 mm flutes | No: deeper than any flute   |
| Design stock                    | Own block 250 × 200 × 80 (not in the library) | 3020 Nova travel 300 × 200 × 80                                                           | Y equals the travel exactly |

So the part is "large" three ways: no stock the user keeps holds it, its cavity is deeper than any tool reaches, and its own block fills the Y travel. Two ways to split it:

1. **One split level** at Z ≈ 30 mm (half the height): a tray (floor + 26 mm of wall) and a ring (30 mm, the cavity goes through). Each layer is ≤ 31 mm deep: within the extralong's flute, and the 1/4″ 1F (31 mm stickout) only just. Needs blanks ≥ 260 × 187 × 31 mm (bounds + 5 mm margins): a wider board or a glued-up panel. **2 pieces.**
2. **The level plus two lines** (X ≈ 125, Y ≈ 88.5) for the library's 200 × 100.1 × 40: pieces of 125 × 88.5 × 30 + margins = 135 × 98.5 (1.6 mm to spare in Y). **8 pieces**, and every line runs through 7 mm walls and the 4.2 mm floor: small end-grain glue faces, no room for a key (DFM [`thinJoint`](#dfm-near-a-split)).

Recommended: (1); (2) gets `thinJoint` on every line. DFYOM supports both. Flow in 4a: add the outline profile first (the part has none yet, so the pieces would be cut along the 250 × 200 stock edges, [`noOutline`](#dfm-near-a-split)) → Split part… → add a level at 30.25 → preview shows two pieces with their blanks and "Fits no library stock; own blank 259.5 × 187 × 30.3" → Done → two pieces under the part → Plan each piece → export or run each.

Until 4a ships, the workaround is one project per piece ([research § D](../research/2026-11-23-multi-setup-alignment.md#d-splitting-one-part-across-several-stocks)): the app allows one part per project.

## Model

### Split

`part.split?: { id, lines: SplitLine[], levels: Length[], margin: Length, joint: JointSpec }` on the **source** part, in the part frame ([design.md § Part and stock](design.md#part-and-stock-v01)).

| Element     | 4a                                                                                    | Later                                                                                                         |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Split line  | Straight, along X or Y (`{ axis: 'x' \| 'y', at }`), through the whole part, vertical | Any angle; an open [named shape](design-v0.3.md#what-a-shape-is) from edge to edge ([4e](#implementation-4e)) |
| Split level | `z` (part frame, between the bottom and the top), horizontal through the whole part   | –                                                                                                             |
| `margin`    | Per side of the blank, default 5 mm (Q68)                                             | Per piece override                                                                                            |
| `joint`     | `butt` (glue faces, no geometry)                                                      | Dowels on levels, puzzle key, dovetail key (4b), finger, half-lap (4d) ([§ Joints](#joints))                  |

`split.detached?: number[]` (4d): the numbers of pieces turned into plain parts by [Detach](#implementation-4d); the source makes no piece there, and new lines or levels drop it.

Lines and levels cut the part's **footprint** into cells: the inside of the part's through outline profile when it has exactly one, else the stock rectangle (finding `noOutline`). Empty cells make no piece.

### Source and pieces

Each non-empty cell is a **piece**: a part record with `derivedFrom: { partId, splitId, index }`, named "<source name> · <n>", numbered by position (X, then Y, then Z, from the front-left bottom). Options for how pieces relate to the source:

| Option                      | Edit after splitting                                         | Cost                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Derived, kept in sync**   | Edit the source; every piece follows in the same command     | Mapping rules below; pieces read-only (like [owned operations, A4–A5](features.md#expansion-and-owned-operations)) |
| One-time copy               | Edit each piece by hand; a source edit means splitting again | Least code; the user's iterations (two scales already) repeat by hand                                              |
| Derived with "Detach" later | As derived; Detach turns a piece into a plain part           | Derived plus one command                                                                                           |

**Recommended: derived, kept in sync** (SP-A, Q109), with Detach as a later option. It follows the feature rule (A1–A2): the expansion is **materialized** (pieces are stored parts, so Plan, export, geometry and DFM work on them unchanged) and **regenerated in the same command** as the source edit (an instant reaction, [apply.md § Rules for new reactions](../architecture/apply.md#rules-for-new-reactions)); piece plans follow by Apply like any design change. A piece keeps, by its index: its name, its blank choice ([§ Nesting](#nesting)) and its operations' tool fields. When a line change removes a piece that has plans, the UI confirms first ("Piece 4 has 1 plan; removing the line removes both"); the command removes them in one undo step.

### Mapping the source into a piece

The piece frame is its blank's front-left top corner; the piece holds `offset` (source part frame → piece frame). Piece operations hold **resolved values** (no expressions; the source keeps them) and keep the source operation ids, so plans read "Pocket 1", not a new name.

| Source operation                               | In the piece                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Removal region outside the piece               | Dropped                                                                                                                             |
| Inside the piece                               | Copied, shifted by `offset`                                                                                                         |
| Crosses a split line (pocket, profile, facing) | Its shape gets an `intersect` item with the piece region plus the tool's clearance ([shape items](design-v0.3.md#shape-definition)) |
| Drilling or hole crossed by a line             | Goes to the piece holding its centre; warning `cutsFeature` (the other piece misses its part)                                       |
| Crosses a level, from the top                  | Upper piece: depth capped at the piece bottom (a through cut). Lower piece: `on` its new top, depth reduced by what is above        |
| Crosses a level, from the bottom               | Mirrored rule                                                                                                                       |
| The through outline profile                    | Replaced by the generated piece outline                                                                                             |
| Side-face operations (±X/±Y)                   | Clipped like a pocket; their face must be an outer face of the piece, else dropped with `cutsFeature`                               |

Generated operations (owned by the split, ids `<splitId>_<role>`): `outline` (a through profile around the piece region, with the plan's tabs) and `glueFace` (a facing to the level on the side a level cut, [§ Blanks and margins](#blanks-and-margins)). Removal regions are 2.5D prisms ([geometry.md](../architecture/geometry.md#25d-evaluation-v01)), so a prism cut by a vertical plane or a Z slab is again a prism: no new geometry kind. Chamfers and floor fillets crossing a level are the exception: warning `cutsFeature`, kept in the piece that holds their face.

**Invariant (tested):** the pieces' evaluated solids, moved back by their offsets, equal the source part's (within the general tolerance), minus nothing and with no overlap.

### Blanks and margins

- A piece's stock is its **blank**: the piece bounds plus `margin` on each side in X and Y; in Z the piece height (4a: own block, no library link, like DF13's [own stock](../architecture/library.md#snapshots)).
- With a thicker blank (nesting onto a library stock, 4c) the piece sits at the **top** of the blank and the surplus is removed in the top setup: by the source's top facing when the piece has the top face, else by its `glueFace` facing (SP-F, Q113). The glue face of an upper piece is then the blank's own bottom (a planed face). This keeps a piece to the setups the source needs; the alternative (surplus at the bottom) adds a flip for every upper piece.

## Joints

| Kind         | Where            | Geometry                                                                                                                           | Clearance                                        | Slice |
| ------------ | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----- |
| Butt         | Lines and levels | None: flat glue faces                                                                                                              | –                                                | 4a    |
| Dowels       | Levels           | Vertical holes in both glue faces at the same X/Y: lower piece from its top, upper piece from its bottom (a flip setup) or through | Hole Ø = dowel Ø + fit (registration hole sizes) | 4b    |
| Puzzle key   | Lines            | Round knob and socket along the line (jigsaw)                                                                                      | Socket offset by the clearance (default 0.15 mm) | 4b    |
| Dovetail key | Lines            | Trapezoid key; inside corners need relief                                                                                          | As puzzle                                        | 4b    |
| Finger       | Lines            | Square wave along the line; inside corners need dogbones                                                                           | As puzzle                                        | 4d    |
| Half-lap     | Lines            | Each piece keeps half the thickness over an overlap; one side cut from the bottom (a flip, [alignment](alignment.md))              | Lap length + clearance                           | 4d    |

- **Clearance** goes on the socket (female) side only, so the male key keeps its nominal size; per joint, default 0.15 mm (S2), and a "Test fit" helper (4b) adds a small two-piece coupon of the joint as its own part so the user tunes the clearance on scrap first.
- **Dogbones:** a router leaves the tool radius in every inside corner seen from the top. The joint generator rounds the male side's outside corners to the tool radius plus the clearance where the shape allows (puzzle, dovetail), else cuts a dogbone (along the bisector) or a T-bone (along one wall, default for finger joints where a bisector would show) sized from the joint's [tool field](design-v0.2.md#operation-tool). DFM warns when no library tool is small enough for the joint's corners.
- **Dowels reuse phase 1** (SP-E, Q112): the dowel size list, the fit (dowel + 0.2 mm) and the drilling or helical-bore toolpaths of the [registration hole](alignment.md#alignment-aids), but as design operations owned by the split, because they are part of the finished part. Horizontal dowels in a vertical glue face (a line) need a side setup, which a hobby machine rarely can hold, so lines get keys, levels get dowels. A dowel hole is also a valid [alignment point](alignment.md#alignment-points) for the piece's own flip setup.

## DFM near a split

Warnings `design.split` with rules (never blocking, as all DFM):

| Rule            | When                                                                                                            | Slice |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----- |
| `noOutline`     | The source has no through outline profile: pieces are cut along the stock edges                                 | 4a    |
| `cutsFeature`   | A line or level crosses a hole, a chamfer, a floor fillet or a side-face operation (mapping table)              | 4a    |
| `featureNear`   | A feature edge lies within 2 × the nearest operation's tool Ø of a line or level (S3): a thin wall at the joint | 4a    |
| `thinJoint`     | A glue face (or a key's neck) narrower than 10 mm or 3 × the joint tool Ø                                       | 4a    |
| `sliver`        | A piece narrower than 2 × margin, or less than 5 % of the part's volume                                         | 4a    |
| `noStock`       | Info: the blank fits no library stock; names the nearest and by how much it is short                            | 4a    |
| `outsideTravel` | The blank is larger than the machine travel of the project's machines                                           | 4a    |
| `fitsWhole`     | Info: the unsplit part fits a library stock and the travel: the split may be unnecessary                        | 4a    |
| `dowelNear`     | A dowel hole's wall to the glue face's edge or a feature within its depth is under 1.5 mm, or it breaks in      | 4b    |
| `dowelFit`      | A level's glue face between two pieces holds fewer than 2 dowels, a hole lies outside it, or its depth is cut   | 4b    |
| `keyFit`        | A run of a keyed line holds no key: too short, only for a tool smaller than the outline's, or no place clear    | 4b-2  |

## Nesting

S4, slice 4c: each piece onto one blank, chosen from the library stocks that hold the piece plus margins (smallest volume first; rotation 0° or 90° about Z), or its own block as in 4a. The choice is per piece and kept across regeneration; a source edit that makes the chosen stock too small turns into `noStock` and falls back to the own block. Several pieces on one sheet needs a plan holding several parts, which plans cannot today; it is a later milestone, not this doc. "Split to fit" (4c): given a library stock and the machine travel, suggest the fewest levels, then lines, that make every piece fit; the user accepts or edits them.

## Plan, export and Run per piece

A piece is a part, so its plan, setups, workholding, export header and Run are today's. Additions: the export header and the Run setup picture name the piece ("Enclosure 1 · 3 of 8"); the plan summary lists the piece's blank; Run has no alignment step for splits (Q68: the joint aligns the pieces at glue-up), but a piece's own flip setup uses phase 1 as any part does. The split shares one need with [tiling (T3)](../plans/proposal-multi-setup-alignment.md#4-phase-3--tiling): clipping an operation to a region. Build it once, here or there, whichever comes first.

## Persistence

`split` on the part and `derivedFrom`/`offset` on a piece are new optional fields: older files load unchanged, so no `schemaVersion` bump ([persistence.md § Schema version](../architecture/persistence.md#schema-version-pre-alpha)). Piece parts are ordinary `parts/<id>.json` records.

## Implementation (4a)

Built 2026-11-24 (task DF91, after S0). Where this doc left room, the decisions taken:

- **Model** (`src/core/design/part.dto.ts`): `part.split = { id: 'split1', lines: [{ axis, at }], levels: [z], margin, joint: { kind: 'butt' } }` (one split per part; `axis: 'x'` is the plane x = at; levels are part-frame z in (−thickness, 0)); a piece has `derivedFrom: { partId, splitId, index }` and `offset: [x, y, z]` (source part frame → piece frame, lengths). Optional fields: no `schemaVersion` bump.
- **Cells** (`split/layout.ts`): the footprint (the one through outside profile on `stock.top` with a closed shape, else the stock rectangle) cut by the lines into rectangles, intersected with the footprint by the 2D port; levels make Z bands from the bottom to the top of the stock, and bands wholly in the top or bottom facing are skipped. Numbering: X, then Y, then Z (bottom first), non-empty cells only.
- **Blank:** cell bounds plus `margin` per side in X and Y, exactly the band height in Z. So **no `glueFace` in 4a**: the blank is the piece height, its glue face is the board face. `glueFace` comes with thicker blanks (4c). The blank is an own block snapshot named "Blank L × W × T" with the source stock's material.
- **Mapping** (`split/pieces.ts`): as the table, plus: resolved literal values; `on` becomes the piece's `stock.top`/`stock.bottom` when the operation starts beyond the piece, else keeps its source surface when that surface's operation is mapped with its floor; otherwise it is dropped with `cutsFeature` (`reason: 'on'`). A pocket capped by a level (through the piece) becomes a **through inside profile** (slot = its tool Ø, 4 tabs): a pocket must end above the stock bottom, and the core is waste held by tabs; likewise a capped profile starts on the piece's stock face. A crossing pocket or profile gets a new named shape `shape<n>`: the source definition plus an `intersect` rectangle of the cell grown by the tool Ø. Drillings go by centre (half-open cells); a drilling capped by a level becomes `through`. A chamfer follows its edge operation; a loop chamfer (any non-circular edge) also carries `sourceEdge`, the source's unclipped edge and the piece's cell, so it bevels only the source's real edges, never a split line ([edge-chamfers.md § Implementation (E2)](edge-chamfers.md#implementation-e2)). **Side-face operations are not mapped in 4a** (`cutsFeature`, `reason: 'sideFace'`). `featureId`/`featureRole` are stripped: pieces hold plain operations. The generated `split1_outline` is a through outside profile (the source outline's shape, slot width, tab count/size and tool, else the cell rectangle, 6 mm slot, 4 tabs).
- **Regeneration** (`split/commands.ts`): `design.split.set` regenerates itself; the `design.splitFollow` document hook regenerates every split source whose record or stock snapshot changed in the command (unchanged records are frozen, `frozen-memo.ts`); the apply hook of the same name runs after the tool choice, so mapped operations carry their source's chosen tool; pieces are left out of the library-change re-choice (`design.toolRechoice`), whose own choice for a smaller piece would never settle against the copied tool (Apply then left keys pending forever). A piece keeps by its number its id, blank snapshot id, a non-default name and its generated operations' tool fields; records are written only when they change. Piece removal with plans: `design.split.set` needs `confirm: true` (`input.malformed`, rule `pieceHasPlans`); a source edit that would drop a piece with plans fails the same way. `design.part.remove` on a source takes its pieces (and their plans, counted for `confirm`); on a piece it fails with `derivedPart`, as every design command on a piece except `design.part.update { name }`.
- **Findings** (`split/findings.ts`): code `design.split` (added to `DFM_WARNING_CODES`) with `params.rule` and `operation` = the split id; returned by `design.split.set` and listed by `design.warning.list` (not by every edit's result, for cost). Absent = butt levels. Optional fields: 4a records load unchanged, no `schemaVersion` bump.
- **Sizes and fit** (`design/dowel.ts`): the dowel sizes 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 mm and the fit `DOWEL_FIT` = 0.2 mm (hole Ø = dowel Ø + fit), shared with the [registration hole](alignment.md#alignment-aids) (its default 6.2 mm is the 6 mm dowel plus the fit). Any dowel Ø from 2 to 20 mm is accepted; the list is what the UI and the placement try. Default depth 2.5 × Ø + 1 mm (a dowel of 5 × Ø with 1 mm of glue room at each end: 6 × 30 mm dowels → 16 mm holes).
- **Where holes go: positions are stored, placed once.** A level with no hole in the args is placed by the helper when `design.split.set` runs (as the alignment-aid helpers place and then store positions, [S17](alignment.md#implementation-slice-2)); a source edit never moves a dowel (the follow stays cheap and positions do not jump), the findings flag one that a source edit made bad. The helper, per level and per column of cells (the lines cut a level into one glue face per pair of pieces): the **valid region** is the cell's footprint minus every removal prism within the hole depth above or below the level (pockets, holes, inside profiles; outside steps intersect; a facing in that range leaves nothing), eroded by the hole radius plus a wall; candidates are points on the eroded region's boundary every 2 mm and a 5 mm grid inside it; holes are picked farthest-point first (the candidate farthest from the region's centre, then the one farthest from every pick), one per 150 mm of the glue face's width + depth, 2 to 8 (the enclosure: 3, so no 180° turn fits). The wall is one dowel Ø where that region spans at least half the glue face in X and Y, else 1.5 mm (`DOWEL_WALL_MIN`). Without a given Ø, the sizes are tried from 6 mm down (6, 5, 4, 3) and the first that spans half the face wins, else the one spanning most. Positions are rounded to 0.01 mm inside an extra 0.05 mm of wall.
- **Holes in the pieces** (`split/dowels.ts`): each hole k makes a generated drilling `<splitId>_dowel<k>` ("Dowel hole k") in **both** pieces at the same X/Y, flat-bottomed, Ø = dowel + fit: the lower piece from its top (`stock.top`), the upper piece **from its bottom** (`stock.bottom`, a `−z` drilling: its plan gets a bottom setup, a flip). **Not through:** a through hole would show on the finished faces (the enclosure's rim and floor); the flip is phase 1's, and the dowel holes are themselves valid alignment points for it ([§ Joints](#joints)). Depth in a piece: `depth`, capped at the blank thickness − 1 mm (`dowelFit`, reason `depth`). The holes sit before the piece's outline (drilled before it is cut free) and keep their tool fields across regeneration like every generated operation; the drilling or helical-bore toolpath and the tool choice are any drilling's, as for the registration hole ([S14](alignment.md#implementation-slice-2)). A hole whose centre is in no cell of its level on either side (in a skipped band or outside the footprint) is dropped from that piece (`dowelFit`, reason `outside`).
- **DFM** (`split/findings.ts`, cached with the geometric pass): `dowelNear` per hole and piece side: the wall (distance from the hole's edge to the valid region's boundary before erosion) under 1.5 mm, or ≤ 0 (it breaks into a feature or out of the face); `dowelFit`: a glue face (piece pair) with fewer than 2 holes (it can turn), reasons `count`, `outside`, `depth`. `thinJoint` at a doweled level uses the joint: limit max(10 mm, hole Ø + 2 × 1.5 mm), message "too narrow for Ø6 dowels". A keyed line (4b-2) keeps the butt rule for its glue face and adds one for its narrowest key neck: under max(10 mm, 3 × the outline's tool Ø), "a weak key" (`params.what: 'keyNeck'`).
- **Agent API:** args on `design.split.set` only: `split.dowels: { diameter?, depth?, holes?: [{ level, at }] } | null` (null or absent = butt levels); a level without given holes is placed by the helper; the result's findings carry the dowel rules. 4b-2 adds `puzzle`/`dovetail` to `joint` and `split.clearance` (refused without a key, `keysOnly`). No place: `keyFit` `near`, no key there (the pieces keep the butt boundary on that run).

A key takes at most a third of its share of the run: on a shorter share it shrinks, down to a neck of tool Ø + 2 mm (the socket's neck lets the tool through); below that no key: `keyFit` `tool` with `maxTool` (the largest tool Ø, 0.1 mm steps, that a key would fit) or `short` (none down to 1 mm).

No new geometry kind. The male piece's blank grows by the knob's depth.

## Implementation (4c)

Designed 2026-11-25 (task DF91) before the code: nesting one piece per library blank (Q115) and Split to fit. Where [§ Blanks and margins](#blanks-and-margins) and [§ Nesting](#nesting) left room, the decisions:

- **Opt-in, per split:** `split.useStocks?: true` ("Use library stocks" in the Split section; `design.split.set { split: { useStocks } }`, absent keeps the stored value). Off, every piece keeps 4a's own block unless the user chose its blank. **Not on by default**: it would change 4a's behaviour behind the user's back (adding any stock to the library would change pieces' blanks, plans and programs; a library board may be another wood; a thicker board adds a facing to the piece). "Split to fit…" turns it on, since picking a stock there says the pieces are cut from library stock.
- **The need** of a piece is 4a's blank: its bounds (with a key's knob) plus `margin` per side in X and Y, the band height in Z. **The automatic choice** (with `useStocks`): the library block stocks of the source stock's **material class** (hardwood, softwood, sheet good, …: the class sets feeds and speeds, and a plywood sheet is no blank for a hardwood part; names may differ within a class) that hold the need, turned 0° or 90° about Z (0° first); the smallest volume wins, ties go to the same material name, then by name. None: the own block, and `noStock` (info) as in 4a.
- **The user's choice** per piece: `design.part.update { partId: <piece>, blank: 'auto' | 'own' | { stockLibraryItemId } }`, the one edit a piece takes besides its name. It is stored on the piece (`blank.choice`) and kept by the piece's number across regeneration, like its name; it applies with `useStocks` on or off. The turn stays automatic (0° if it fits, else 90°): a forced turn waits for a grain field on stock ([open questions](#open-questions-for-the-docs-not-the-user)). A choice that does not hold the piece, or an id that is no library stock, is refused at once (`input.malformed`, rules `blankTooSmall`, `unknownItem`). A later source edit that makes the chosen stock too small (or a chosen stock removed from the library) falls back to the own block with `noStock` as a **warning** naming the chosen stock and how much it is short; the choice stays, so the piece returns to it once it fits again.
- **Record** (`part.dto.ts`, on a piece): `blank?: { libraryItemId?, rotation?: 90, choice?: { kind: 'own' } | { kind: 'library', libraryItemId } }`: where the blank comes from (no `libraryItemId` = own block) and the user's choice (absent = automatic). Optional fields, no `schemaVersion` bump.
- **A turned board, not a turned piece:** at 90° the blank snapshot's length and width are swapped; the piece frame and every operation stay as in 4a (no operation rotates). **Blank snapshots stay unlinked** (no `libraryItemId`), as 4a's: a piece is derived and follows its choice at every regeneration (source edits, Apply, its own blank choice), not by "Update from library"; a turned board is not the library's values, which a linked snapshot would report as `libraryChanged` (and an update would turn it back). The link is `piece.blank.libraryItemId`. The snapshot holds the library stock's values (name, material, notes) with the turned sizes.
- **Where the piece sits:** centred in X and Y on the blank; in Z the piece's bottom on the blank's bottom with the **surplus** s = blank thickness − band height above it. This is SP-F read with its own mechanics ("faced off in the top setup"; "the glue face of an upper piece is then the blank's own bottom"): the surplus lies above the piece, and the piece is machined from the top as its source is. `offset` z is −(band top + s).
- **Removing the surplus:** every operation of the piece that starts at the piece's top from above (`+z`, mapped onto `stock.top`, dowel holes of a lower piece included) goes s deeper from the blank's top. So a mapped top facing of the source removes the surplus in its own passes (SP-F, "by the source's top facing"); a piece without one gets a generated facing `<split>_glueFace` first ("Glue face"; "Top face" when the piece holds the source's top), depth s. Operations from the top then start at the blank's top and cut s of air after the facing (a small cost; starting them on the facing's floor is a later refinement). Through cuts (the outline, keys) go through the whole blank; tabs stand on the blank's bottom as before; operations from the bottom are unchanged.
- **Findings** (`split/findings.ts`): `noStock` as above (info: no library stock holds the piece; warning: the chosen one no longer does); `outsideTravel` checks the piece's actual blank (a library board larger than the travel warns); `fitsWhole` unchanged.
- **Split to fit** (`split/suggest.ts`): `design.split.suggest { partId, stockLibraryItemId?, machineLibraryItemId? }`, a query (changes nothing). A piece's need must fit the **box**: the stock (turned 0° or 90°) and the machine travel (the given machine; else the project's plans' machines, else the library's; one is enough); a stock alone or a machine alone works; neither: `input.malformed` (rule `nothingToFit`). **Levels:** the fewest bands no thicker than the box, evenly over the source stock's thickness (the enclosure's 80 mm in 40 mm stock: one level at −40). **Lines:** for 0, 1, 2, … lines (at most 20 per axis), every grid nx × ny with nx + ny − 2 lines, evenly spaced over the footprint's bounds; a grid is checked on the real layout (each cell's bounds plus margins, the knob reach when the lines are keyed); among the grids with the fewest lines the **stoutest** wins (the largest smallest piece side, so no strips: the enclosure in 3 × 1 would leave its middle upper piece as two loose walls), then fewer pieces. Positions are rounded to whole millimetres when the grid still fits, else to 0.01 mm. The margin is the split's (else 5 mm / 0.2"). The result holds `lines` and `levels` as sources to pass to `design.split.set`, the piece count and the largest need; nothing fits within 20 lines and 20 levels: `input.malformed` (rule `noFit`). Lines are not moved off features: `featureNear` and the drag do that after.
- **UI** ([ui.md § Split part](../conventions/ui.md#split-part-split-parts-4a)): each piece chip names its blank ("Block 200 × 100.1 × 40", "· turned", or "own blank") and opens a menu to change it (Automatic, Own block, the library stocks of the class that hold it, smallest first); the switch "Use library stocks"; **Split to fit…** (with or without a split) lists the library stocks of the source's material class and applies the suggestion with `design.split.set` (lines and levels replaced, `useStocks` on, margin and joint kept, dowel holes placed again); the piece's inspector names its blank. The plan inspector of a piece's plan names the blank ([§ Plan, export and Run per piece](#plan-export-and-run-per-piece)).
- **Agent API:** `design.part.update { blank }`, `design.split.set { split.useStocks }` and the `design.split.suggest` query: **107,440** chars (+757, 139 tools), so 4c is +0.76 K of the ≈ 0.8 K left and phase 4 +2.93 K of its ≈ 3 K ([§ Agent API](#agent-api), [llm-api.md](../architecture/llm-api.md#implementation-task-310)).
- **Exit (headless, `exit-criteria.split.fixture.ts`):** the enclosure with a library stock "Block 200 × 100.1 × 40" (Eucalyptus): `design.split.suggest` gives the level at −40 and the lines x 125, y 100 (8 pieces, need 134.75 × 98.5 × 40); applied with `useStocks`, every piece is on that library blank (programs "Stock X 200 Y 100.1 Z 40"), planned with no errors, exported and run on the virtual machine. Hidden e2e: Split to fit… on the enclosure, the chips name the library blank.
- **Tests:** `split/blanks.test.ts` (the choice: smallest volume, class, turn; the user's choice kept by number, refused when too small or unknown, the fallback with `noStock` after a source edit; surplus: the source's top facing deepened, else the generated glue face, dowel holes deeper, offsets; reassembly with surplus; a surplus piece valid on its blank), `split/suggest.test.ts` (the enclosure's level and lines, the stoutest grid, travel only, keys, `noFit`, `nothingToFit`), `split-view.test.ts` and `SplitParts.test.tsx` (chips, the blank menu, the switch, Split to fit…, axe).

## Implementation (4d)

Designed 2026-11-25 (task DF91, hardening) before the code; the user delegated the choices (D70: the recommended options, **accepted by delegation**, the user may veto). [§ Slices](#slices) named three items for 4d: split lines as shapes, finger and half-lap joints, Detach. **4d builds the two joints and Detach**; lines as shapes move to [4e](#slices): a curved or polyline line makes the cells general regions instead of rectangles, and the mapping, keys, dowel faces, blanks, the exploded preview and the drag all assume rectangles today (`Cell.rect`), so it is a slice of its own, not an add-on. Where [§ Joints](#joints) left room, the decisions:

- **Model:** `joint.kind` gains `finger` and `halfLap` (the lines' joint, as puzzle and dovetail); `clearance` applies to every joint but butt (same default 0.15 mm, socket side only; `keysOnly` now reads "a clearance needs a keyed, finger or half-lap joint"). `split.detached?: number[]` lists detached pieces by number. Optional values, no `schemaVersion` bump.
- **Finger joint** (`split/keys.ts`, the key generator): per run of each line, as keys, square **fingers** of the male (lower) piece into the female, with the female's fingers in the gaps. Width p and depth p: nominal max(10 mm, 3 × the outline's tool Ø, band height / 2) (the key neck rule, so a nominal finger never warns `thinJoint`); n = ⌊(run / p − 1) / 2⌋ fingers, then p = run / (2n + 1), so the run starts and ends with a female finger (the outline's corners stay whole). A run shorter than 3 nominal p takes one finger of run / 3, down to tool Ø + 2 mm, else `keyFit` (`tool` with `maxTool`, or `short`). The fingers need the same clear place as a key (no removal within one tool Ø around each finger through the band), else no finger on that run (`keyFit` `near`, butt there). Each finger is a `Key` (`size.kind: 'finger'`), so the clearance, the knob reach on the male blank, the Test fit coupon (one finger and two gaps), the key read-out and the preview come with it.
- **Corner relief: T-bones** (as [§ Joints](#joints) says for fingers). A router leaves its radius in each inside corner seen from the top: the male's at the finger's base, the female's at the socket's bottom. Each gets a half-round notch of radius f = tool radius + clearance centred on the **finger's cheek** at f from the corner (the male's into the finger, the female's into its own finger beside the socket), so the outline tool reaches the corner exactly and the cheeks, the glue faces, stay straight from the shoulder up; the notch shows as a half circle from the top, as any T-bone. Items: per finger a closed path `add` (male) or `subtract` (female, grown by the clearance at the tip and the cheeks) and two circles as closed paths of four quarter arcs (`subtract`), after the outline's `intersect` with the cell, as keys. The read-out's largest tool is min(2f, p).
- **Half lap** (`split/laps.ts`): per pair of pieces across a line in a band, the male (lower) piece keeps the **bottom half** over a lap of length ℓ centred on the line and the female the **top half**: ℓ = max(10 mm, 3 × tool Ø, 2 × band height between the facings), the lap plane at the middle of that band. The male's outline region grows ℓ / 2 past the line (its cell rectangle, so the source outline still clips it), the female's ℓ / 2 − clearance back over it; generated pockets `<split>_lap<k>` ("Half lap k") take the halves off: the male's from its top (`stock.top`, depth to the lap plane, plus the surplus of a thicker blank) over ℓ plus a run-out of tool radius + 1 mm past the lap's far end and along the line past both ends of the cell, the female's **from its bottom** (`stock.bottom`, a flip as for dowel holes) over ℓ + clearance and the same run-outs. The shoulders run edge to edge, so no inside corner seen from the top needs relief (the pocket walls meet the floor square, which a flat end mill cuts); the pockets' own corners are rounded to tool radius + 0.5 mm and lie in the run-out, outside the piece. The overlap (ℓ + clearance + one tool Ø around) must be clear of every removal through the band: else no lap there, `keyFit` `near`, butt. **Lines on one axis only:** where an X and a Y line cross, four laps would claim the same corner at two heights, so `design.split.set` refuses `halfLap` with lines on both axes (`model.invariant`, rule `lapCrossing`, path `split.joint`: "a half lap needs lines along one axis only (X or Y)"); laps at crossings are 4e. No Test fit for laps (4e): the coupon is the key's.
- **Detach** (Q109 option 3): `design.part.update { partId: <piece>, detach: true }` turns a piece into a plain part in one undo step: it loses `derivedFrom`, `offset` and `blank`, keeps its name, blank snapshot, operations (generated ones too, now ordinary; the ids stay), shapes and plans, and its number goes into the source's `split.detached`, so the source no longer makes that piece (its cell stays in the layout: the neighbours keep their joint with it). A plain part refuses it (`pieceOnly`). Changing the split's lines or levels drops `detached` (the numbers mean other cells then): those cells make pieces again, the detached parts stay. Undo brings the piece back. The piece inspector gets **Detach** beside Edit the split.
- **Findings:** `keyFit` names the joint (`params.joint` `finger` or `halfLap`, `params.lap` = ℓ for a lap): "No fingers on the line at X 125 mm between pieces 1 and 2 at Y 0–100 mm: a feature or the part edge is within one tool Ø (6 mm) of a finger. A butt joint there; move the split or the feature.", "No half lap on … : a feature or the part edge lies within the 18 mm lap or one tool Ø (6 mm) of it. …"; `thinJoint` with `what: 'fingerWidth'` ("The fingers on … are 8 mm wide, less than 18 mm …: weak fingers"). The T-bones and laps need no new rule.
- **UI** ([ui.md § Split part](../conventions/ui.md#split-part-split-parts-4a)): "Lines joined by" becomes a **select** (Butt, Puzzle, Dovetail, Finger, Half lap; five do not fit a segmented control in the inspector, the new two with a second line saying what they do); the settings fold under a one-line summary as before: "Finger settings · clearance 0.15 mm · 10 fingers", "Lap settings · clearance 0.15 mm · lap 36 mm"; the clearance field reads "Joint clearance" for fingers and laps; read-outs "10 fingers · 26.1 mm wide · T-bones for the Ø6 mm outline tool" and "1 half lap · 36 mm overlap · the upper piece is cut from its bottom (a flip)"; Test fit for fingers, not laps. The top preview draws the fingers as keys and each lap's overlap as a closed outline. **Detach** beside Edit the split in the piece inspector.
- **Agent API:** two enum values on `design.split.set`'s `joint`, the clearance description and `detach` on `design.part.update`: **113,949** chars (+119 over 113,830, 141 tools; [§ Agent API](#agent-api)).
- **Tests:** `split/joints.test.ts` (finger layout; every inside corner of both pieces ≥ tool radius, for Ø3, Ø6 and Ø6.35, the pieces disjoint; the panel's 10 fingers and half lap with its two pockets, the female's on `stock.bottom`; `lapCrossing`; findings; Test fit on fingers; Detach and undo), `reassembly.test.ts` (fingers and the half lap reassemble the source within the clearance band and T-bones, the lap in 3D), `plan/split-joint-toolpaths.test.ts` (a 190 × 90 board with a finger joint and with a half lap: every piece planned with no error, generated, each setup exported, **goldens** `plan/__golden__/split-{finger,lap}-*.nc`, and streamed through the virtual machine; the lap pockets stop on the lap plane), renderer `dfm.test.ts` and `SplitParts.test.tsx` (the select, settings, read-outs, no Test fit for a lap, Detach, axe). Existing goldens unchanged.
- **Exit (headless, `exit-criteria.split.fixture.ts` via `dfyom mcp`):** a 1000 × 300 mm shelf on a 1010 × 310 × 18 board, longer than the test machine's 800 mm travel, split at x = 505 with fingers: no `keyFit`, `thinJoint` or `outsideTravel`, both pieces within the travel, planned with no errors, exported and run on the virtual machine; from the saved project, the pieces' outline regions moved back by their offsets cover the shelf's outline once (no point in two pieces), the misses (clearance band and T-bones) under 0.5 % of its area. Hidden e2e: Finger from the joint select on the enclosure (no room, its read-out), then Butt.

## Implementation (4e)

Designed 2026-11-25 (task DF91, hardening) before the code, built the same day; the user delegated the choices (D70: the recommended options, **accepted by delegation**, the user may veto). [§ Slices](#slices) named four items for 4e: split lines as shapes, half laps where lines cross, Test fit for laps, edge chamfers at the lap ends. **4e builds split lines as shapes**; the three lap items move to [4f](#slices) (they touch the lap generator only, and a shape line is the larger, riskier change). Where [§ Split](#split) said "an open named shape from edge to edge (4e)", the decisions:

- **Model** (`part.dto.ts`): a split line is `{ axis, at }` (4a) **or `{ shape: shapeId }`**: an open [named shape](design-v0.3.md#what-a-shape-is) of the source part, read in the part frame's top view (shape x, y = part X, Y in mm; no placement, rotation or mirror). Options were (1) a reference to a named shape, (2) the path stored inline on the line, (3) a copy taken when picked. **(1) recommended and taken:** the Shape Editor already draws and edits open paths of lines and arcs (handles, segment table, one undo step per edit), the pieces follow a shape edit like any source edit ([SP-A](#source-and-pieces)), and the tool's arg stays one small object (the ≤ 0.3 K budget). Optional value, no `schemaVersion` bump.
- **What a shape line must be** (`splitLinePaths`: `design.split.set` and every source edit that regenerates the pieces refuse otherwise; `model.invariant`, path `split.lines.<i>`): the shape exists and resolves to one open path (rule `lineShape`); both ends on or outside the stock rectangle (within 0.01 mm), so the line runs edge to edge (`lineEnds`); the path does not cross itself (`lineCrossing`); the same shape twice is `duplicate`. A shape a split line uses cannot be removed (`design.referenced`, issue path `split.lines.<i>`, as an operation's shape). Lines may cross each other (shape or straight) any number of times. A half lap refuses shape lines (`lapCrossing` reads "a half lap needs straight lines along one axis only (X or Y)").
- **Cells as regions** (`split/layout.ts`): each line cuts the stock rectangle into its two **sides**: the path, its ends carried out to the nearest side of a box 10 mm around the stock, closed round that box counter-clockwise (the left side of the path's direction). The cells are the connected regions of the stock that one side of every line makes, each intersected with the footprint per band as in 4a. A cell keeps its **cut** (the region before the footprint) and its cut's bounds as `rect`. **A split whose lines are all straight keeps 4a's rectangle code exactly** (same cells, numbers, pieces and programs: no golden changes); one shape line switches the whole layout to regions (straight lines then are paths: the X line drawn +Y, the Y line drawn −X). Numbering: Z band (bottom first), then the cut's bounds centre Y, then X.
- **Mapping:** the piece outline is the source outline (or the cell bounds without one) with an `intersect` closed path item of the cut (lines and arcs, to 1 µm, an arc's radius rounded up), then the key items, as in 4b-2. Mapped pockets, profiles, patterns and chamfers are judged by their bounds box: one wholly inside the cut is copied unclipped, one not within its tool Ø of the cut is dropped, the rest clip to the cut's bounds rectangle as 4a clips to its cell (exact inside the piece, the rest cut in the blank's waste outside the piece outline: a small toolpath cost; an exact clip is later). A drilling goes to the first cell of its band whose cut holds its centre; a centre on a line goes to the piece past it in +X, else +Y (as 4a's half-open cells). A loop chamfer crossing a shape line bevels the source edge within the cell's bounds (E2's `sourceEdge.within` is a rectangle): past the line that is waste.
- **Joints on a shape line** (`split/keys.ts`, `planPathKeys`): butt everywhere. **Puzzle, dovetail and finger keys on its straight segments only**: each segment is sampled every 0.5 mm for the pair of cells on its two sides (inside the footprint), each run of one pair is sized and placed by the 4b-2 / 4d rules (`placeRun`, shared with straight lines) in the segment's frame (`Key.frame`: origin, direction, normal into the female); the male piece is on the **left** of the line's direction (so straight lines keep their 4b-2 sides). **Arcs take no key:** a key's root on an arc leaves a sliver of the sagitta between knob and line, so a pair of pieces that meets only along arcs gets `keyFit` reason `curved` ("No key on split line 1 between pieces 1 and 2: they meet along arcs only, and joints go on straight segments. A butt joint there; give the line a straight stretch."). Options were (1) straight segments only, (2) keys bent to the arc, (3) refuse keyed joints on shape lines; (1) keeps 4b-2's proven geometry (socket = knob + clearance, inside corners ≥ tool radius) and still keys a curved joint wherever the user draws a straight run. Knobs and sockets are path items as before, rotated into the frame (mirrored arcs flip their sense). Keys and their findings carry `line` (the index) and, on a shape line, `shape`. Dowels on levels work on any cells (the glue face is the cell's region).
- **DFM for shape lines:** `cutsFeature` (a hole the path passes within its radius), `featureNear` (a removal's bounds within 2 × its tool Ø of the path, `params.nearLines` = line indices), `keyFit` and the key neck's `thinJoint` as 4b-2, by `params.line` and `shapeId` instead of axis and value. **Not yet:** the glue face along a shape line is not sampled for `thinJoint` (straight lines' still are), and a sharp corner of the path is not flagged although the outline tool rounds it on the concave piece only (draw tangent arcs; both are 4f).
- **Blanks:** a piece's need is the cell's bounds plus its knobs' outlines (any direction) plus the margins, as 4c; Split to fit suggests straight lines only, as before.
- **UI** ([ui.md § Split part](../conventions/ui.md#split-part-split-parts-4a)): a shape line's row in the Split section holds a select "Line along shape" of the part's open shapes (one already used by another line disabled), **Edit shape** (the Shape Editor) and "Remove line along <shape>"; findings and the key count under it as for a straight line. A second add row: **Add curved line** sends `design.shape.add` (an open path "Split curve n" across the part at its middle along Y: two straight runs and a bump of four tangent arcs, so no sharp corner) and `design.split.set` with one gesture id (one undo step); **Line along shape** (shown while an open shape is not yet a line) adds a line along the first such shape. The Top preview draws each piece's footprint from its cell and the lines' paths; the 3D exploded preview pulls pieces apart along straight lines and levels only, and a shape line has no plane handle (it is edited in the Shape Editor).
- **Agent API:** `design.split.set`'s line is `{ axis, at } | { shape }` (the shape made with `design.shape.add`, an open `path` item); the summary says "lines (straight or an open shape)", `design.shape.remove`'s "an operation or split line". Measured: **114,198** chars (+249 over 113,949, 141 tools; [§ Agent API](#agent-api)).
- **Tests:** `split/shape-lines.test.ts` (a curved line's two region cells: their union the footprint, no overlap, numbering; the refusals `lineShape`, `lineEnds`, `lineCrossing`, the shape in use; a shape edit regenerates the pieces; on the table, keys on the polyline's straight runs only, each piece valid on its blank, every inside corner of its outline ≥ the tool radius, and the four outlines moved back cover the disc once with misses under 0.2 % (the clearance band); `curved` on an arc-only line; a half lap refused; dowels on a level under the curve, a hole on the line to one piece per band, `cutsFeature`), `plan/split-joint-toolpaths.test.ts` (a 190 × 90 board split by a shape with a slanted straight run and a tangent arc, puzzle keys: both pieces planned with no error, generated, exported, **goldens** `plan/__golden__/split-curve-{1,2}-top.nc`, streamed through the virtual machine; the outline's items: the cell, the cut, the knob or socket), renderer `split-view.test.ts` (args, no drag handle, findings by index, the seed) and `SplitParts.test.tsx` (Add curved line as one undo step, the row, remove, axe). Existing goldens unchanged.
- **Exit (headless, `exit-criteria.split.fixture.ts` via `dfyom mcp`):** a round table top Ø 1200 mm (a 1210 × 1210 × 25 oak board, larger than the test machine's 800 mm travel, a through hole and a pocket the curve crosses) split by a curved line (the named shape `TABLE_CURVE`: x = 605 with a bump of four R 60 tangent arcs to x = 485) and the straight line y = 605, with puzzle keys: four pieces, no `keyFit` or `outsideTravel` (the 65 mm run from the bump to y = 605 takes a small key: a `thinJoint` warning), each planned with no errors, exported and run on the virtual machine to Idle; from the saved project the pieces' outline regions moved back by their offsets cover the table once, the misses (the clearance band) under 0.5 % of its area.

## Questions for the user

Answered 2026-11-24 (TG #317 "go"): every recommendation below was taken.

1. **Q109 – Pieces follow the source.** (1) Derived and kept in sync, read-only; (2) one-time copy; (3) derived, with Detach later. _Recommendation: (1), Detach later (3)._ You iterate on the enclosure (two scales already); a copy would make every change a re-split by hand.
2. **Q110 – What 4a ships.** (1) Straight lines **and levels**, butt joints, margins, own blanks; (2) lines only, as the proposal first said; (3) (1) plus dowels on levels. _Recommendation: (1)._ Your enclosure needs a level (60.5 mm tall, cavity deeper than your longest flute); lines alone would not help it.
3. **Q111 – Several parts in the app.** (1) Lift `onePart`, a part menu in Design with pieces under their source, plans pick their part; (2) keep one part per project and write each piece to its own project. _Recommendation: (1);_ your file already holds two parts and the app shows only one.
4. **Q112 – Dowel holes.** (1) Design operations owned by the split, reusing the registration hole's sizes and toolpaths; (2) plan aids in each piece's plan, as the proposal said. _Recommendation: (1);_ they are part of the finished part and must match in both pieces, so they belong to the design.
5. **Q113 – Thicker blank.** (1) Piece at the blank's top, surplus faced off in the top setup; (2) piece at the bottom, surplus faced from below (a flip). _Recommendation: (1);_ fewer setups, and the glue face is the board's planed bottom.
6. **Q114 – First keyed joint (4b).** (1) Puzzle key; (2) dovetail key; (3) both. _Recommendation: (3);_ same generator, the dovetail is the puzzle with straight flanks.
7. **Q115 – Nesting scope.** (1) One piece per blank from the library (4c), several pieces per sheet as a later milestone; (2) several per sheet now. _Recommendation: (1);_ several per sheet needs plans of several parts, a larger change than this phase.
8. **Q116 – Default margin.** (1) 5 mm per side (Q68); (2) tool Ø + 2 mm, from the outline's tool. _Recommendation: (1)_ for 4a; (2) as the default once nesting picks stock, since it saves material on small tools.
