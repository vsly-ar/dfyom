# Heatsink presets for patterns: straight fins, pin fins, radial fins

Status: accepted (2026-11-27; hardening [DF136](../plans/hardening.md#intake-user-feedback-goes-here), the user's TG #445 after cutting aluminium parts successfully: "please plan adding a few patterns (presets) to the pattern feature, suitable for heatsink fins"; [Q201–Q210](#questions-for-the-user) take their recommendations, accepted by delegation D86). H1 implemented ([§ Implementation (H1)](#implementation-h1)); H2–H4 open · Builds on: [patterns.md](patterns.md) (the pattern feature, its lattices, invert, DFM and the Presets dialog of [DF107](patterns.md#presets-dialog-and-more-presets-df107)), [roughing.md](roughing.md), [plan-v0.3.md § Pocket](plan-v0.3.md#pocket-offset-clearing-of-a-region), [feeds-speeds.md § Hobby metal cutting (DF131)](../architecture/feeds-speeds.md#hobby-metal-cutting-hardening-df131), [plan-v0.1.md § Minimum stickout (DF130)](plan-v0.1.md#minimum-stickout-hardening-df130) · Terms: [glossary.md](../glossary.md)

A machined heatsink is a block with parallel **slots** (straight fins), a grid of **pins** left standing (pin fins), or slots running out from a hub (radial fins). Each of these is a pattern: a lattice of cells cut by one pocket, as recesses (slots) or inverted so the cells stand as islands (pins). The pattern feature already has every piece except three: open-ended slots with a set outer wall, a radial lattice, and a pocket that cuts a slot exactly as wide as the tool in one pass per level. This doc adds a **Heatsink** group to the Presets dialog, a short parameter step that fills the pattern's fields from fin terms (fin thickness, slot width, fin height), the DFM that matters for thin aluminium fins on a hobby router, and those three additions.

## Mapping onto the pattern model

Fin terms and pattern fields. "Gap" in the pattern is the **web** between cells, so for straight fins the pattern's Gap is the **fin thickness** and the cell's width is the **slot width**; the parameter step exists so nobody has to make that swap in their head.

| Preset                 | Cell                                                                           | Lattice                                         | Spacing (gap = web)      | Invert | Margin             | Fit                                                      | Existing kind?                                                            |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------ | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Straight fins**      | rectangle, width = slot, length = the area's length along the fins, corner R 0 | square, one row                                 | gap = fin thickness      | no     | `[wall, 0]`        | `whole`                                                  | yes, plus `margin: [u, v]` (HS-C) and the slot rule (HS-E)                |
| **Square pin fins**    | rectangle pin × pin, R 0                                                       | square                                          | gap = channel (≥ tool Ø) | yes    | 0 (open all round) | `whole`                                                  | yes, unchanged (a cross-cut heatsink: the channels are the two slot sets) |
| **Round pin fins**     | circle Ø pin                                                                   | square                                          | gap = channel            | yes    | 0                  | `whole`                                                  | yes, unchanged                                                            |
| **Staggered pin fins** | circle Ø pin                                                                   | staggered (the gap is exact for circles)        | gap = channel            | yes    | 0                  | `whole`                                                  | yes, unchanged                                                            |
| **Radial fins**        | rectangle, width = slot, from the hub radius to one slot width past the rim    | `radial`, `count`, `radius` = the slots' middle | – (count sets the pitch) | no     | 0                  | `clip` (every slot crosses the rim alike, so no slivers) | no: the `radial` lattice (HS-D)                                           |

- **Cross-cut / diamond pins** (straight fins cut a second time across, or at 45°) give the same square pins as the inverted square preset, which cuts them in one pocket, level by level. Two crossing slot patterns also work today, but the second one cuts full-height fins into pins with nothing beside them, so the preset is the inverted one. Diamond pins are the square preset with the cell turned 45° on a staggered lattice; not a card of its own (ask if wanted).
- **Counts fit the area.** The layout keeps every cell wholly inside the area less the margin and is symmetric about the origin, so the count is odd. The parameter step computes the largest count n with n·slot + (n − 1)·fin ≤ width − 2·wall; when n is even it sets the origin half a pitch off the centre so the slots stay centred. The read-out shows the resulting outer wall.
- **Worked example (the first slice):** a 40 × 40 mm aluminium block, fins 1.5 mm, slots 2 mm, 10 mm deep, outer wall 1.5 mm: n·2 + (n − 1)·1.5 ≤ 37 gives **n = 11 slots, 12 fins** (10 inner and 2 outer walls of 1.5 mm), 11 × 2 + 10 × 1.5 + 2 × 1.5 = 40 exactly. That exact fit is the containment test's edge case and is tested ([slices](#slices)). Removed volume 11 × 2 × 40 × 10 = 8 800 mm³.
- **Round stock:** DFYOM's stock is a box. A round heatsink is a round part (an outline profile or a circle boundary shape) on a plate; the radial preset centres on the boundary's centre (the default origin). Turning or a rotary axis is out of scope ([engraved-patterns.md § Round parts](engraved-patterns.md#round-parts) made the same call).

## What a preset stores

- **The parameter step's values are not stored.** Applying fills `cell`, `lattice`, `spacing` (or `count` and `radius`), `alternate: none`, `invert`, `margin`, `fit`, `origin` and `depth` in **one `design.pattern.update`** (one undo step), as the 13 decorative presets fill their four fields. Afterwards the pattern is an ordinary pattern: editing Gap changes the fin thickness, Depth the fin height.
- **Sizes taken from the part at apply time** (the area's length for straight fins, the boundary radius for radial fins) are filled as numbers. A later stock or boundary change does not follow; the DFM catches what goes wrong (`dfm.patternEmpty` when slots no longer fit, closed slot ends shown in the viewport). Linking would need a stored heatsink sub-model ([Q203](#questions-for-the-user) option 3).
- **Heatsink read-out (derived, not stored):** when a pattern matches a heatsink shape (slot cells in one row, or inverted pins), the read-out reads "11 slots · 12 fins 1.5 mm · 10 mm high (1:6.7) · base 4 mm" instead of "11 cells". It is computed from the fields and the stock, so it is always true.
- **Current marking:** the dialog marks a heatsink card "Current" when the pattern has that card's cell kind, lattice and invert (sizes are the user's), so reopening the dialog lands on it with the step pre-filled from the fields.

## Model changes

- **`margin: length | [u, v]`** (HS-C), in the `on` face's frame. The area is the boundary eroded by an axis-aligned box of half-sizes u, v: the intersection of the boundary moved by ±u along the face's u and ±v along its v, through the 2D port (exact for any boundary; for one value it stays today's offset, so existing records are byte-equal). `[1.5 mm, 0]` keeps 1.5 mm walls at the sides and lets the slots run out at both ends. Margin 0 at a stock edge is already allowed ([patterns.md § Boundary and clipping](patterns.md#boundary-and-clipping)); the pocket is then open at the slot ends and the open-side rules of [plan-v0.3.md](plan-v0.3.md) apply. Invariant: each value ≥ 0 (`nonNegative`).
- **Lattice `radial`** (HS-D): `count` (integer ≥ 2) cells evenly spaced on a circle of `radius` about the origin, starting at `angle`, each turned by its node's direction − 90° plus the cell's own rotation, so a cell's length points outwards (the code of `engraving/resolve.ts`'s radial turn moves to `pattern/layout.ts` and is shared). `spacing` is not used and is kept for when the lattice changes back. `patternOverlap` still applies (neighbours at the hub must not overlap). `alternate: flip` is refused on `radial` (nothing to nest).
- **No other new fields.** Fin height is `depth`; the base is the stock under it (`lessThanStockThickness` unchanged). No migration and no `schemaVersion` bump: two optional shapes of existing fields and one enum value (pre-alpha rule).

## DFM: heatsinks in aluminium

Findings from the hobby-router case (a 3020–6040-class machine, 0.5–0.8 kW spindle, carbide 1–3 flute end mills, [DF131](../architecture/feeds-speeds.md#hobby-metal-cutting-hardening-df131) feeds). Each says whether DFYOM already checks it.

| Concern                                  | Rule of thumb                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | In DFYOM                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fin thickness vs height**              | A free-standing aluminium wall chatters and bends above about **1:8–1:10** (thickness : height) while the cutter loads it. The example is 1.5 : 10 = 1:6.7, fine; 1 mm fins at 10 mm (1:10) are at the limit                                                                                   | **New `dfm.patternTallWeb`** (warning, at Depth; inverted: at Cell): depth / narrowest web (pins: narrowest island) > 8. The message suggests the depth at 8 × web and the gap (fin) at depth / 8. The web is the one `dfm.patternThinWeb` already measures, so it costs nothing extra. One limit for all materials ([Q205](#questions-for-the-user)) |
| **Slot width vs tool Ø**                 | Slot (gap between fins) ≥ tool Ø. Slot = Ø gives a full-width slot (one pass, no finishing pass, walls as the tool leaves them; runout makes the slot ≈ 0.02–0.05 mm wider, the fins that much thinner). Slot ≥ Ø + 2 × allowance (2.4 mm for Ø 2 at 0.2) gives a finishing pass on both walls | Exists: tool fit's `width` rule and `dfm.pocketNoToolFits` (the cell is narrower than every library end mill). The parameter step offers the library's flat end mills ≤ the slot and says which case applies ([Q206](#questions-for-the-user))                                                                                                        |
| **Fin height vs flute length and reach** | Depth ≤ flute length, else the shank rubs the wall. Small end mills deflect with reach: depth over 3–4 × Ø (10 mm with Ø 2 is 5 × Ø) needs light stepdowns and a short stickout. A common Ø 2 has 6–8 mm of flute; 10 mm fins need a long-flute Ø 2 (≥ 10 mm) or a reduced-neck one            | Exists: `shankRub` ([DF75](plan-v0.1.md#finishing-defaults-hardening-df75-df76), plan finding, pocket depth > flute unless the neck is reduced) and DF130's minimum stickout (10 mm + the collet nut clearance). The parameter step reads "10 mm deep needs ≥ 10 mm of flute" against the chosen tool ([Q207](#questions-for-the-user))               |
| **Thin walls and finishing**             | Cut every slot level by level (all slots at one Z before the next), so a fin is never cut on one side while full height and free on the other. Finish a thin wall in short steps, not one full-height pass                                                                                     | The pattern pocket already cuts **level by level across all cells** (`region-pocket.ts`). The finishing ring (slot > Ø only) runs in levels of `finishStepdown` ([DF76](plan-v0.1.md#finishing-defaults-hardening-df75-df76)); [Q208](#questions-for-the-user) adds a hint to lower it on tall webs                                                   |
| **Climb vs conventional**                | A full-width slot cuts climb on one wall and conventional on the other: the choice does not apply ([plan-v0.3.md T-E](plan-v0.3.md#decisions)). Finishing passes in aluminium: climb (thin chip at exit, no rubbing), the pocket default                                                       | No change: the pocket's `direction` stays climb                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Chips in deep narrow slots**           | Chips pack in a 2 mm slot 10 mm deep and get recut: air blast or mist, 1–2 flutes ([`feeds.manyFlutes`](../architecture/feeds-speeds.md#hobby-metal-cutting-hardening-df131) warns at 3+)                                                                                                      | Exists for flutes. Air blast is a note in the parameter step (DFYOM has no coolant model)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Tabs**                                 | Not applicable: a pocket leaves nothing loose                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | –                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Pins**                                 | A pin is a wall loaded from every side: the same 1:8 limit on its smallest width. A channel exactly the tool's Ø is contoured round each pin, so every channel is cut twice (once per neighbour)                                                                                               | `dfm.patternTallWeb` on the narrowest island. The parameter step suggests a channel of Ø + 0.5 mm so the second pass finishes instead of cutting air                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Radial fins**                          | Fins are thinnest at the hub: fin(r) = 2πr / count − slot. The count follows from the fin thickness wanted at the hub                                                                                                                                                                          | `dfm.patternThinWeb` and `dfm.patternTallWeb` measure the web at the hub (the narrowest)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

**Time.** At DF131's metal stepdown (0.05–0.08 × D, 0.1–0.16 mm for Ø 2) the 10 mm need 63–100 levels: 11 slots × 40 mm × 63–100 levels ≈ 28–44 m of full-width slotting. Today's ring cuts each slot twice per level (≈ 55–88 m, half of it in air), which is why the slot rule comes with the first slice. The plan's estimate is the measure, recorded in H1.

## Plan

| Preset                          | Operation that cuts it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Roughing (DF108/DF122)                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Straight fins, slot = tool Ø    | The pattern pocket, **slot rule** (HS-E): per level, each slot once along its centre line, alternating direction, slots in nearest-neighbour order; open ends are entered from outside the stock at the level's Z (no ramp); a closed slot ramps back along its centre line as the Line does ([engraved-patterns.md § Toolpath](engraved-patterns.md#toolpath-plan)). | Pins (square, round, staggered) | The inverted pattern pocket: offset clearing of the channels, open to the stock edges with margin 0, a finishing ring round each pin                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Only for the open border when the margin leaves room; usually no gain                                                                         |
| Radial fins                     | As straight fins (slot rule when slot = Ø); clipped at the boundary circle, so the slots open at the rim once the part's outline profile cuts it free                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | As straight fins                                                                                                                              |

- **The slot rule** (`toolpath/region-pocket.ts`): a piece of the wall region that exists only through the `TOOL_FIT_TOLERANCE` fallback (the tool is up to 0.01 mm wider than the cell) and whose sliver is straight (its two long sides parallel within the tolerance) is a **slot piece**. Its centre line replaces its ring tree. Other slivers keep today's ring. It is generic: any pocket cell as wide as its tool, not only patterns. Feeds: the full-slot engagement the pocket already assumes ([feeds-speeds.md § Engagement](../architecture/feeds-speeds.md)), DF131 stepdown.
- **Order** stays level by level across all cells (the thin-wall rule above); slots within a level go nearest-neighbour, so the tool zigzags across the block.
- **Facing first:** a heatsink face should be flat before the slots; the parameter step does not add a facing (it is a separate operation the user may already have).

## Testing

- **Layout:** `margin: [u, v]` against the box-erosion oracle (a rectangle boundary: the area is the rectangle less u and v; a circle boundary: the intersection of four shifted circles); one-value margins byte-equal to today. The exact-fit case (11 slots in 37 mm). `radial`: count, angles, cell turn, `patternOverlap` at the hub, `flip` refused.
- **Presets:** each heatsink preset's fill for the worked sizes (the args of one update), the even-count origin shift, the parameter step's count and read-out as pure functions (`pattern/heatsink.ts`, headless).
- **Slot rule:** a single straight slot as wide as the tool, open at both ends and closed: one centre-line pass per level, alternating, no move outside the slot's tool-centre region, no finishing ring, cut length = slot length × levels (± entries); a slot 0.02 mm wider than the tool still uses the rule, 0.03 mm falls back to rings. Existing pocket goldens byte-equal.
- **DFM:** `dfm.patternTallWeb` at 1:8 ± ε, for slots, pins and radial (hub web).
- **Exit (H1):** see [Slices](#slices).

## Implementation (H1)

Built 2026-11-27 (DF136 H1). Decisions made while building, by delegation:

- **Fins run along the face's v (Y).** The slot cell is a rectangle `along` × slot turned 90°, the lattice one row of `square`; `margin: [wall, 0]`. `pattern/heatsink.ts` (headless) holds the step: `straightFins` (count, actual wall, even-count origin shift, ratio, base), `straightFinsArgs` (the args of one `design.pattern.update`), `slotCut` (the step's slot note) and `straightFinsReadout` (the derived read-out: a sharp rectangle at least twice as long as wide, turned by a multiple of 90°, square lattice with a gap, not inverted or through, too long for a second row).
- **`margin: [u, v]`** (`layout.ts` `shrinkBy`): one value keeps the round offset (byte-equal); a pair erodes the boundary by the box of half-sizes u, v as the intersection of the boundary moved by ±u, then ±v, which is exact for convex boundaries (a rectangle, a circle) and a close inner bound for concave ones. Each value ≥ 0 (`model.invariant` at `margin.0` / `margin.1`). The command takes a quantity or a pair; an update without `margin` keeps the pair.
- **The slot rule lives in geometry** (`geometry/slots.ts`, `slotSplit`): a piece that is a sharp four-line rectangle without holes, as wide as the tool within `SLOT_FIT_TOLERANCE` (= the pocket's `TOOL_FIT_TOLERANCE`, 0.01 mm) on each side and longer than wide, is a slot. An end whose two corners lie on or beyond the stock edge is **open**: the centre line runs past it by r √½ + the finishing allowance (DF61's `openReach` − r), the tool steps down there at the plunge rate. A closed end stops a radius inside and is entered by a zigzag ramp along the centre line at the ramp angle. `inputs.ts` splits the slots out **before** the open region is built (the open region of a slot as wide as the tool would cut only air); not with a keep-out, partial material, roughing or analytic contours. The pocket generator cuts the slots after the rings of each level, nearest end first (so the tool zigzags across the block), with a rapid down to 0.5 mm above the level into the slot already cut. No finishing ring. The DFM rest-material check takes the same slots out first (`shape-measure.ts`), so the user's heatsink shows no `dfm.restMaterial` (the open region left 24 mm² "uncut" outside the stock at the outer walls before).
- **Goldens:** every existing golden is byte-equal: the rule only takes pieces exactly as wide as the tool, which no earlier golden has.
- **`dfm.patternTallWeb`** (`pattern.ts` `tallWebOf`): the narrowest web at depth / 8 (the web search of `patternWeb`); skipped when the pattern is through or the web is already thin (`dfm.patternThinWeb` speaks); parameter `depth` (fins) or `cell` (pins), params `web`, `ratio`, `suggestedDepth` (⌊8 × web⌋), `gap` (⌈depth / 8⌉).
- **Dialog:** `PresetsDialog` gained a `header` (the Decorative · Heatsink control, label "Preset group"), `onChoose` (a heatsink card opens its step instead of applying), `step` (shown in place of the grid with its own footer, Back and Apply; Escape goes back first), an `icon` per card and controlled `open`. The dialog opens on the group of the current preset, else the group last used this session (kept with the inspector's remembered steps). The cards' grid still takes the focus on open. The step's tool list is the library's flat end mills ≤ the slot + 0.02 mm, the largest first; Apply sends `design.pattern.update` and `design.operation.tool.set` (that tool, locked) with one gesture: one undo step. The step starts from the user's sizes (1.5 / 2 / 10 / 1.5 mm) each time; pre-filling from the current pattern waits for H2.
- **Inspector:** Margin's **Per side** splits it into **Margin across (U)** and **Margin along (V)**; **One margin** sends the across value for both. The read-out becomes the heatsink read-out when `straightFinsReadout` matches.
- **Q208's hint** (finish thin walls in steps of ≤ 3 mm) stays in H2 as the slice table has it: a straight fin cut by the slot rule has no finishing pass, so H1's heatsink has nothing to hint.
- **Measured (the user's heatsink):** 11 slots, 12 fins, open at both ends; removed volume 8 800 mm³ (within 0.2 %); at the 0.16 mm stepdown of a DF131 aluminium preset, 63 levels, each slot once per level (693 passes), no ramp, no finishing ring; **cut length 29.4 m, estimated time 87 min** (the ring cut ≈ twice that, half in air). A Ø 2 with 6 mm flutes gives `shankRub` (and `dfm.reach`); 1 mm fins give `dfm.patternTallWeb` (1:10, depth ≤ 8 mm or fins ≥ 1.25 mm). Golden `src/core/plan/__golden__/heatsink-straight-fins.nc` streams through the virtual machine. Tests: `pattern/heatsink.test.ts`, `geometry/slots.test.ts`, `plan/heatsink-toolpaths.test.ts`, `PatternPresetsDialog.test.tsx`, `e2e/heatsink.e2e.ts` (hidden; `DFYOM_H1_DIR` captures the step and the part, light and dark).
- **Calculator gap:** a Ø 2 in aluminium has no calculator feeds (DF39/DF131, `feeds.noData`); the plan needs a tool preset or entered feeds, as the exit test's tool has.

## Questions for the user

Numbering continues after [cut-state.md](cut-state.md#questions-for-the-user)'s Q200. "Q201 ok" takes the recommendation.

1. **Q201 – How straight fins are cut.** (1) **Pattern slot cells cut by the pattern pocket, plus the slot rule** (a cell exactly as wide as the tool is cut once per level on its centre line). (2) The engraved pattern's Line with a straight-line motif and a flat end mill: it already zigzags, but it cuts each line to full depth before the next, so fins are cut free on one side (it would need a level-first order). (3) A new "grooves" pattern kind (patterns.md Q124's grooves). _Recommendation: (1)._ It keeps one feature, level-by-level order and the existing DFM, and the slot rule helps every pocket as wide as its tool.
2. **Q202 – Open slot ends.** (1) **`margin: [u, v]`:** walls across the fins, 0 along them. (2) The preset draws a boundary shape for the user (a named shape with the walls). (3) Closed slots only (one margin all round). _Recommendation: (1)._ Air must flow along the fins; a per-axis margin is a small, exact model change, and a preset cannot create shapes.
3. **Q203 – What a preset stores.** (1) **Fills the fields once from a parameter step** (fin thickness, slot, height, wall); a derived heatsink read-out in the inspector. (2) Fixed-number cards like the 13 decorative presets, no step. (3) A stored, linked heatsink sub-model that re-derives the pattern on every edit and follows stock changes. _Recommendation: (1)._ The fields map one to one onto fin terms, so a link adds a second truth for nothing; the step does the one non-obvious part (the count and the gap/fin swap).
4. **Q204 – Radial fins.** (1) **A `radial` lattice** (`count`, `radius`, cells face out), in its own slice. (2) Polar lattice plus the engraving's radial rotation, with the pitch computed for the count (rings and the origin cell get in the way). (3) Later. _Recommendation: (1), as H3._ One ring of N cells is what radial fins and bolt-circle-like slot rings need; polar's ring rule cannot set a count.
5. **Q205 – Fin aspect limit.** (1) **`dfm.patternTallWeb` warning above 8:1** (height : narrowest web) for every material. (2) Per material (aluminium 8, brass 10, plastics 6, wood 5). (3) No finding, a note in the parameter step only. _Recommendation: (1)._ 8:1 is the conservative end of the 1:8–1:10 hobby rule; a material table can come with the material work (as the 1 mm web limit, [patterns.md § Decided here](patterns.md#decided-here-not-questions)).
6. **Q206 – Slot width and the library.** (1) **The step offers the library's flat end mills ≤ the slot** and states "one pass per level" (slot = Ø) or "finishing pass on both walls" (slot ≥ Ø + 2 × allowance); between the two, a note that the finishing pass is thinner than the allowance. (2) Require slot ≥ Ø + 2 × allowance always (a finishing pass always). (3) Ignore the library. _Recommendation: (1)._ Your case (2 mm slot, Ø 2) is the common hobby way and must work; a finishing pass is an option, not a must.
7. **Q207 – Deep slots with small tools.** (1) **The existing `shankRub` and DF130 minimum stickout,** plus the step's read-out against the chosen tool's flute length. (2) Also a new reach warning (depth > 4 × Ø for tools ≤ 3 mm in metals). (3) The step caps the height at the tool's flute length. _Recommendation: (1)._ The findings already exist at the right place (Plan); a reach rule needs data DFYOM does not have yet (deflection limits per tool).
8. **Q208 – Finishing thin walls** (only when slot > Ø). (1) Automatic: on webs under 3 mm, finish at every roughing level. (2) **A plan hint** on a pattern pocket with a tall web: "Finish in steps of ≤ 3 mm" with a fix that sets `finishStepdown`. (3) Nothing. _Recommendation: (2)._ It uses the existing knob and changes no default program.
9. **Q209 – Where the gallery lives.** (1) **A Heatsink group in the existing Presets dialog** (segmented Decorative · Heatsink, icons per preset, a parameter step in the card). (2) A separate "Heatsink…" dialog. (3) Add → Heatsink, which adds a pattern with the step. _Recommendation: (1)._ One place for presets, one look; Add → Heatsink can come later if heatsinks become common.
10. **Q210 – Agent API.** (1) **No preset argument:** `margin: [u, v]` and `radial` only, the recipe in the tool description (≈ +0.2 K, to ≈ 118.6 K). (2) A `preset` + parameters argument on `design.pattern.add` (≈ +0.6 K). (3) A `design.pattern.presets` list tool (≈ +0.4 K). _Recommendation: (1)._ The budget is 1.6 K from the limit; agents compute five fields easily and `dryRun` shows the result.
