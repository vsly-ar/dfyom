## Chamfer toolpath

Input: the chamfer's axis point (the edge centre in the face frame), the edge diameter d, the chamfer diameter D (edge Ø + 2 × width, D45), the included angle α, the face Z; the tool's DC, tip diameter dt and angle. A V-bit's cutting cone of half-angle α/2 touches the chamfer cone along its whole slant only when the angles match (the fit rule, [design-v0.4.md § Chamfer operation](design-v0.4.md#chamfer-operation-v04-circular-edges)).

- **Plunge** (DC ≥ D and dt ≤ d): rapid to the centre at the clearance height, feed down at `plungeRate` to the Z where the tool's cone has diameter D at the face (tip Z = face − (D − dt) / 2 / tan(α / 2)), short dwell optional (strategy `dwell`, default 0), rapid out. The hole below lets the tip pass through the edge diameter without cutting.
- **Contour** (DC < D): the tool axis runs one circle of radius r so the cutting cone meets the chamfer from d to D. With slant height s = (D − d) / 2 / sin(α / 2) and usable cutting length l of the tool's cone, the fit rule guarantees s ≤ l, so one ring at full depth cuts it (several rings would be stepped clearing, later). Entry: a ramp along the first circle from the hole side (inside the edge diameter, which is already cut), climb direction, arcs as `G2`/`G3` ([plan-v0.3.md T-B](plan-v0.3.md#decisions)); exit: lift on a tangent arc into the hole.
- **Order checks:** a chamfer must cut after its `edgeOf` operation in the same setup ([order checks](plan-v0.1.md#plan-operations-and-order)); `plan.order` blocks the reverse.
- **Strategy parameters:** `entry` (`plunge`, `contour`, `auto` = P-C), `dwell`, `rampAngle` (contour); nothing else. A finishing pass is not offered (one ring cuts to size). As built ([Implementation (T1)](#implementation-t1)): `auto` is the default (no stored value), `dwell` is later.

### Stepped chamfer (F7)

A chamfer with a `stepdown` (the stepped countersink, [design-v0.4.md § Stepped countersink](design-v0.4.md#stepped-countersink-f7-d41), D41) is cut by a **flat end mill** as terraces; Plan resolves its `entry` to `stepped` (a stored `plunge` or `contour` is `strategy.entry` `stepped`), and `rampAngle` applies.

- **Terraces:** N = ⌈h / stepdown⌉ of height t = h / N (h the chamfer depth), terrace k a circle of radius r<sub>k</sub> = D / 2 − (k − 1)·t·tan(α / 2) at depth k·t, top to bottom, so every top step corner lies on the cone.
- **Rings per terrace** (tool radius R): the outermost has its axis at r<sub>k</sub> − R; inner rings (spacing ≤ DC / 2, equal) until the innermost, at most d / 2 + 0.4·DC, reaches into the hole. Usually one ring: an end mill that bores the hole has DC > d / 2.
- **Moves:** rapid to the first terrace's innermost ring start (−90°) at clearance and retract, feed down to the face at `plungeRate` (inside the hole); per terrace a **ramp along the innermost ring** from the level above to k·t (whole turns, at most `rampAngle`, never descending faster than `plungeRate`; a straight plunge below r = 0.1 mm), one full ring at k·t, then each outer ring after a radial feed outward; then a radial feed back in to the next terrace's innermost ring (cleared). After the last terrace, feed to the centre (the hole) and rapid out. Climb (counter-clockwise inside the hole), one pass of kind `rough`.
- **Checks:** tool type `flatEndMill`, DC < d (`plan.check` `diameter`), reach to h; the generator re-checks type, size and the terrace count (≤ 1000).
- **Feeds:** the end mill's helical-boring engagement `helix { ap }` with ap = t (the hole's helix uses the same tool), drilling's field list.
- **One tool:** the drilling and the chamfer get the same design tool ([design-v0.4.md § Stepped countersink](design-v0.4.md#stepped-countersink-f7-d41)), and no other tool comes between them: the default order puts a chamfer that shares its edge operation's tool **right after that operation** (ahead of the pockets of other tools), and rebuild moves a new or re-resolved one up behind it ([Order](#implementation-t1)); a Clones of stepped countersunk holes plans every bore, then every terrace set, as one tool block (D34). Only a user's own reorder of kept operations can still put a tool between them.

### Chamfers wider than the cutting edge (hardening DF142)

Status: implemented (2026-11-28; hardening [DF142](../plans/hardening.md#2-dogfood-intake), user TG #488; accepted by delegation, the TG #488 brief; [§ Implementation (DF142)](#implementation-df142)). Supersedes the "one ring" rule of [P-C](#decisions) and of [design-v0.4.md § Implementation (C2)](design-v0.4.md#implementation-c2) for circular edges.

**Problem.** An M6 countersink (Ø6.4 → Ø13.84, 90°) asked for a Ø12 chamfer mill, which does not fit an ER11 collet; the user's 1/4″ 90° V-bit was refused ("The 90° V-bit 1/4″ does not fit") although a 1/4″ end mill bores the hole first. The cause was the v0.4 fit rule: a circular chamfer is plunged (DC ≥ D) or cut on **one ring**, which needs a cutting edge at least the chamfer's slant (D − d ≤ DC − dt; here 7.44 > 6.35). Several rings were kept for later because rings from the inside out "drag the shank through uncut stock". That is only true when each ring goes to full depth: edge chamfers have cut a bevel wider than the cutting edge in **levels** since [E4a](edge-chamfers.md#implementation-e4a), with the body above the cone only ever in cleared air. A circle is the same case with the hole as the air.

**Decision.**

- **Fit** (`chamferFit`, circular form). Angle within 0.5° and tip < edge Ø as before; then the chamfer fits when the tool **plunges** it (DC ≥ D), cuts it in **one ring** (cutting edge ≥ slant), or else cuts it in **levels** when both hold:
  - **Shank** (rule `shank`): the shank is no wider than the cone's largest Ø, S ≤ DC + 0.01 mm (S = the library's shank Ø, else DC: a V-bit's shank is its DC). Above DC the tool is the shank, and in levels the shank goes below the face by h − (DC − dt) / (2·tan(α/2)).
  - **Bore** (rule `bore`): the material inside the edge is removed by the edge operation. A drilling (drill or helical boring, whose end mill is wider than d / 2, so no core) and a circle pocket clear it; an **inside circle profile** cuts only a ring and leaves its core (a slug held by tabs, or an island when blind), so it does not. The geometry input marks such an edge `edgeCore: true` (absent for drillings and pockets, so their inputs stay byte-identical).
- **Toolpath** (`generateChamfer`, entry `contour`). One ring when the cutting edge covers the slant (unchanged, byte-equal). Else the rings of [E4a](edge-chamfers.md#implementation-e4a) `edgeChamferPasses` with width w = (D − d) / 2: n levels of tip depth z<sub>k</sub> = k·h / n, each from the **edge ring** (axis radius (d − dt) / 2, the tip on the edge) out in equal steps ≤ c = (DC − dt) / 2 to the **bevel ring** (axis radius d / 2 + w − z<sub>k</sub>·t − dt / 2, its cone on the final countersink); the last level is the one ring. Moves: down into the hole at the edge ring's start (−90°) to the face as today; per level a **ramp along the edge ring** from the level above (whole turns, at most `rampAngle`, never faster than `plungeRate`), a full ring, then each outer ring after a radial feed outward at depth (within the cutting edge, as E4b's step-in), and a radial feed back to the edge ring (cleared); after the last level the tangent half circle into the centre and out. Inner rings are `rough`, bevel rings `finish`. A recut chamfer drops the rings in its as-cut cone (`withoutAsCut`, as E4a).
- **Why the shank never enters material.** (1) Inside the edge Ø it is in the bore: the edge operation is cut first (the default order puts it first; the reverse is `plan.order` `edgeBefore`, an error) and reaches below the chamfer (else `dfm.chamferTooDeep`). (2) Outside, at level k the shank's bottom is at z<sub>k</sub> − c / t ≤ z<sub>k−1</sub> − cusp (Δ + cusp = c / t), where level k − 1 has cleared everything between its edge ring and its bevel ring, whose cone lies on the final surface; the shank's farthest point from the axis of the hole, on the edge ring, is (d − dt + S) / 2 ≤ the final countersink radius at the shank's bottom, d / 2 + c, exactly when S ≤ DC. The heightmap test checks it with the bore pre-cut.
- **Removal solid** unchanged (every ring's cone lies inside the countersink, the E4a argument). **Feeds:** unchanged, `chamfer { depth }` at the full chamfer depth with De capped at DC (F10); F6's rectangle is an upper bound, conservative as for E4a levels.
- **Auto choice.** Among library tools that fit, one that cuts the chamfer **in one pass** (plunge or one ring) ranks before one that needs levels (a new key after the Line keys); the rest of the ranking is unchanged. Generalized tools are tried only when no library tool fits, as before. So the user's library V-bit is taken over a generalized Ø12 mill, and a library chamfer mill that covers the chamfer still wins over a smaller V-bit.
- **Messages.** `dfm.toolUnfit` gets texts for the chamfer rules (`angle`, `tip`, `slant`, `shank`, `bore`; they fell back to "does not fit"). `dfm.chamferNoTool` names the reason in a `reason` param: `angle` (no chamfer mill of that angle: the old text), `tip`, `shank` or `bore` (the first failed rule of the library's chamfer mills of the angle, in that order of preference: bore, shank, tip).
- **Edge chamfers** (general loops) get the same `shank` rule for levels; the E4a argument assumed it too.
- **Not changed.** Nothing stored, no `schemaVersion` bump; the plunge and one-ring paths and their goldens; stepped chamfers (F7).

## Feeds for chamfer mills

Amendment to [feeds-speeds.md](../architecture/feeds-speeds.md), applied with task C1 ([§ Chamfer mills](../architecture/feeds-speeds.md#chamfer-mills-v04), formula F10, engagement `chamfer { depth }`): the cutting speed uses the effective diameter `De = dt + 2 · depth · tan(α / 2)` at the deepest point (≤ DC), with the same chip-load data as a flat end mill of that diameter for the material class; spindle speed from De, feed from the tool's flutes. ## Implementation (DF142)

[§ Chamfers wider than the cutting edge](#chamfers-wider-than-the-cutting-edge-hardening-df142) as built, 2026-11-28.

- **Fit** (`geometry/tool-fit.ts`): `chamferFit` adds rules `shank` and `bore` after the one-pass test `chamferInOnePass` (exported for the ranking); the circular `ChamferFitInput` takes `edgeCore`. - **Generator** (`toolpath/chamfer.ts`): `chamferLevels` (E4a's `edgeChamferPasses` with w = (D − d) / 2) and `levelMoves`; one level keeps the old one-ring code path, so its output is byte-equal. The old `slant` refusal becomes `bore` (path `operation.edgeCore`) for a contour wider than the cutting edge on an edge with a core.
- **Auto choice** (`design/tool-choice.ts`): the ranking key `levels` (one pass first) after the Line keys.
- **DFM** (`geometry/dfm.ts`): `dfm.chamferNoTool` carries `reason` (`angle`, `bore`, `shank`, `tip`, `slant`), for circular and general edges.
- **Messages** (`renderer/src/messages/dfm.ts`): `dfm.toolUnfit` texts for `angle`, `tip`, `slant`, `shank`, `bore`; `dfm.chamferNoTool` by `reason`.
- **Chamfer feeds fields (TG #490, same session).** The Plan inspector offered Stepdown and Stepover on a chamfer, and `plan.operation.update` refused them (`notForKind`, a raw error). A chamfer has no stepdown concept to route them to: its levels follow from the tool's cutting edge (Δ = c / t − cusp), and a stepped chamfer's terrace height is the design's `csStepdown`. So the fields are not shown: `feedFieldsOf(kind)` (`plan/plan.dto.ts`) is the one list for core's refusal and the inspector's `feedFields` (drilling and chamfer: spindle speed, feed rate, plunge rate). A smaller depth per level for a chamfer (a max stepdown on the levels) is later. Component test `ChamferFeeds.test.tsx`: every Feeds field shown for a countersink is entered and stored through the real runtime, and no Stepdown or Stepover shows.
- **Order:** unchanged; the default order already puts the edge's plan operation first (edge dependency), proven for the M6 case by the plan test.
- **Tests.** `toolpath/chamfer-levels.test.ts`: the M6 countersink Ø6.4 → Ø13.84 with a 1/4″ 90° V-bit (3 levels of 1.24 mm, rings 2, 2, 1) over a heightmap with the bore cut first: gouge 0, leftover < 0.05 mm, the shank (Ø DC above the cone) never over material; a shank of Ø8 is caught by the same oracle; a tipped 60° bit, conventional; the `bore` refusal; a recut drops rings; golden `chamfer-levels-m6.txt`. `geometry/tool-fit.test.ts` (levels, `shank`, `bore`, one pass), `design/countersink-levels.test.ts` (the M6 hole takes the library V-bit after the 1/4″ end mill; a Ø16 chamfer mill still wins; `reason` `shank` and `bore`), `plan/countersink-levels.test.ts` (bore first, V-bit, entry `contour`, no plan errors, exported to Z −3.72), `messages/dfm.test.ts`. `chamfer.test.ts`: a cutting edge just short of the slant now cuts in levels (it was refused, `slant`).
- **Goldens:** all existing goldens byte-equal (their chamfers are plunged or fit one ring); one new golden.
- **Known gaps:** the feeds keep the full chamfer depth as engagement (F6 rectangle, conservative: a levelled countersink may report more power than it needs); no max stepdown for the levels (the step follows from the cutting edge); a through inside circle profile without tabs whose slug falls free is still treated as leaving its core.

## Machining order v2

Status: implemented (X1 follow-up "plan order v2"). Amends the default order and the loose-piece check of [plan-v0.1.md § Plan operations and order](plan-v0.1.md#plan-operations-and-order); the dependency rules (`onBefore`, [`edgeBefore`](#implementation-t1)) are unchanged. X1 showed the v0.1 order (group by kind, then tool within a group) changing tools 5 times for the lid's 3 tools, and cutting the outline before the openings inside it. Standard CAM practice: cut tool by tool, and the cut that frees the part last.

| Rule                | Behaviour                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Freeing cut         | A **through profile** (side `inside` or `outside`, or `on` without a depth) frees the piece inside its shape. In the default order it comes after every plan operation of its face direction that lies inside it: another operation by its centre (a facing always), another profile by its centre when that profile's bounding box is smaller (two profiles never wait for each other). "Inside" is the loose-piece test (circle by radius, else bounding box; general shapes by their placed box, with the 2D port only). A chamfer on the profile's own edge (an inside circle profile, X2) is not inside it: it bevels the rim round the opening, and `edgeBefore` puts it after the profile anyway. A freeing dependency that would close a cycle with the hard ones is dropped.                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Release cut last    | Hardening DF57 (the agent's two-setup enclosure test cut the outline before the back rim rebate, so the rebate was cut on a part held only by tabs; the rebate's centre lay outside the outline's box, so the freeing rule above missed it). A **release profile** is a through profile that no other through profile of its face direction frees (by the rule above): the outermost one, which separates the part from the stock (the outline, not a cutout inside it). In the default order it frees **every** other plan operation of its face direction, wherever it lies, except the other release profiles (two outlines never wait for each other) and a chamfer on its own edge; so it comes last in its setup. Tabs or not: a release without tabs frees the part all the same. Inner cutouts keep the inside rule, so they still come before the outline but may share runs with other operations.                                                  |
| Fewest tool changes | The order is a sequence of **tool runs**. A run takes every operation of its tool as soon as it is ready (an extra operation joining the current run never makes the later runs longer), so only the tool of each next run is chosen: `defaultOrder` searches those choices for the fewest runs (depth first, memoised on the set of done operations; past 5000 runs explored it takes the first candidate). Candidates are tried in the order of their first ready operation (kind group: facing, drilling, pockets, chamfers, profiles; then design order), and the first minimal order found is kept, so the result is deterministic. An operation without a tool is a run of its own.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Setup boundary      | Hardening DF65 (the user's real two-setup cut changed tools at the re-fixture although the bottom setup could start with the tool still in the spindle). `deriveSetups` orders the setups first, then each setup after the first with the **last tool of the setup before it** as the preferred first tool (`defaultOrder`'s `startTool`): when an order with the fewest runs can start with that tool, that one is taken, so the tool stays in the spindle across the re-fixture and the setup has one tool change fewer. It is a tie-break only: it never adds a run to the setup and never overrides a rule (dependencies, freeing cuts, the release cut last); when no minimal order starts with that tool, the order is unchanged. Only the first run is affected: the setup's last tool is not chosen to suit the next setup (the release cut usually fixes it anyway). Applies to the default order (`plan.create`); a rebuild keeps the user's order. |
| Within a run        | Kind group, then design order among the ready operations (drillings before pockets of the same tool, a drilling on a counterbore floor right after its counterbores, profiles last); a chamfer sharing its edge operation's tool (F7) right behind that operation. Clones stay tool by tool (D34): every instance shares its source operation's tool, so they join one run. A plan operation's passes already go roughing before finishing; there is no separate finishing operation to order.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Rebuild placement   | New plan operations are placed by group first (as before, so `repairOrder` sees them); once their tools are assigned, each moves to the position in its valid range that adds the fewest tool changes (`placeByTool`; ties: nearest its group position). The range: after its dependencies (a freeing profile also after the operations inside it), before the operations depending on it and before a freeing profile enclosing it, unless the user's order already cut that profile earlier. Kept operations keep the user's order; `keepWithEdge` (F7) runs after it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Check               | `plan.loosePiece` (warning, rule `profileFirst`) looks at through profiles only (a blind groove frees nothing) and now also at a later profile inside one (the smaller-box rule above): a user order that cuts the outline before the cutout and vents inside it warns once per later plan operation. It stays a warning: tabs hold the part, and a user may order so on purpose. Since DF57 it also warns, rule `releaseFirst`, for every later plan operation of the direction after a release profile ("Outline releases the part before Back rim rebate: the part is held only by its tabs").                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Tool choice         | Not a Plan rule: the counterbore of a hole takes the hole's end mill when it fits ([design-v0.4.md § Counterbore tool](design-v0.4.md#counterbore-tool)), so a counterbored hole needs no second end mill. Tools are otherwise unchanged (largest that fits, [design-v0.2.md § Auto choice](design-v0.2.md#auto-choice)); the order absorbs the rest.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

Code: `src/core/plan/order.ts` (`defaultOrder` with `startTool`, `freeingDependencies`, `releaseProfiles`, `placeByTool`, `orderFindings`), `setups.ts` and `build.ts` pass the 2D port. The lid of X1 had 2 tool changes for 3 tools: Flat 3 (countersink holes, counterbores, holes below them, vents) → chamfer mill (countersinks) → Flat 6 (cutout, outline). With X2's chamfer on the cutout's edge (it follows the cutout) the fewest runs are Flat 3 → Flat 6 (cutout) → chamfer mill (countersinks, the cutout's chamfer) → Flat 6 (outline): **3 tool changes for 3 tools**, the only order with 4 runs. ## Tool changes per setup (hardening DF66)

Status: implemented. The user asked to see a setup's tool changes in the Plan inspector and to reorder them by dragging (real cut, TG #242). A setup's machining order is a sequence of **tool runs** (consecutive plan operations with the same tool copy; [Machining order v2](#machining-order-v2)); each run starts with a tool change.

| Part         | Behaviour                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Runs         | `toolRuns(operations)` (`src/core/plan/tool-runs.ts`): the setup's operations split where the tool copy changes; an operation without a tool is a run of its own. Each run: tool (snapshot id or none), its plan operations in order, the index of its first operation.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Tool changes | A setup's tool changes count every run, **the one at the setup start included** (the operator loads the first tool after re-fixturing), less one when the first run's tool is the last tool of the setup before it in machining order (`carriedTool`: it stays in the spindle across the re-fixture, DF65). The exported program's `toolChanges` stat differs: it counts the pauses after the first tool, which is loaded before the program starts ([grbl.md § Input boundary](../architecture/grbl.md#input-boundary), First tool); the inspector counts what the operator swaps.                                                                                                      |
| Reorder      | A run moves as a block: `plan.operation.move { count }` moves `count` consecutive plan operations starting at `planOperationId` (the run) to `toIndex` (the block's first position after the move), in one command and one undo step. The command's checks are unchanged: it refuses with `plan.order` when the move adds an order violation (an `on` dependency, a chamfer's edge), and a move that cuts the release profile earlier only warns (`plan.loosePiece`, rule `releaseFirst`), as a single operation's move. ## Disabling in a plan (DF106)

Status: implemented, with its UI and kept plan operations (hardening DF106, user TG #349: "if possible and low-risk, we can also support disabling operations in the plan mode, which only works one way (disabling/enabling in the Design mode transfers the state to the Plan, but not backwards)"; decided by delegation, D70) · Design side: [design.md § Disabling operations and features](design.md#disabling-operations-and-features-df106)

- **One way.** Design's disabled items never reach a plan: the snapshot copies the enabled part ([design.md](design.md#disabling-operations-and-features-df106)). A plan may switch off more on its own: `PlanDTO.disabled` (design operation and feature ids, top-level as in Design; optional, absent = none), which Design never reads.
- **Effect.** The plan's `design.part` is its snapshot without those items and their dependants (`enabledPart(taken, plan.disabled)`), so their plan operations, toolpaths, simulation and program are gone like those of a design-disabled item; `design.taken` keeps them, so they come back. The list is not a plan-only edit: `hasPlanEdits` leaves the plan's own disabled items out of both sides, so a rebuild needs no `discardPlanEdits` for them.
- **Kept across rebuilds.** A draft's follow on Apply and `plan.rebuild` take the current (enabled) design and apply the plan's list again; ids the design no longer has are ignored.
- **Toggling** (`plan.update { planId, disabled }`, replaces the list, `draft` and `settledDesign` as other plan edits) rebuilds the plan **from its own snapshot**, its plan edits kept: it never takes design changes (rebuilding stays deliberate, [plan-v0.1.md](plan-v0.1.md#plans-are-snapshots)). Ids are checked against `design.taken` (`unknownItem`, `insideFeature` as in Design).
- **Plan operations are kept while off** (DF106 follow-up, decided by delegation, D70: keep, not re-create). A rebuild (a draft's follow, `plan.rebuild`, a toggle) that leaves out every design operation of a plan operation because they are **off** (in the design or in this plan, but still in the design) parks it in `PlanDTO.parked` (`{ setupId, index, operation, edits? }`, optional, absent = none) instead of dropping it; its tool copy stays. When they are back, the same plan operation returns – id, tool, preset, overrides, locks, strategy, Z reference – at its index in its setup (if the setup is gone or faces another way: placed as a new operation, settings kept), then the setup order is repaired; a `design` tool is resolved again, a plan-assigned tool stays. A settled plan's own edits of the parked design operations (`edits`) come back on a toggle; a rebuild from the design discards them like any plan edit. A parked plan operation whose design operations left the design is dropped on the next rebuild. Enabling therefore restores the plan as it was (core test `disabled-plan.test.ts`).
- **Stale indicators:** in a settled plan whose snapshot still has an item that Design switched off since, `plan.stale.list` reports `disabledInDesign` (not `removedFromDesign`); the row shows **Off in design** instead of "Removed from design", and a rebuild parks its plan operation.
- **UI** (built; decided by delegation, D70): a plan operation's row menu (right-click, `Shift+F10`) ends with **Disable in this plan**, which switches off the top-level design items it machines (an owned operation switches its feature; an alignment aid has none). The navigator ends the setups with a muted list **Off** ([ui.md](../conventions/ui.md#design-and-plan-controls-v02)): items off in this plan with the marker **Off in this plan** and the row menu **Enable in this plan**, then items switched off in Design with a dashed **Off in design** marker and no menu (enabled in Design only, one way). Each toggle is one `plan.update`, announced ("Drilling 1 is off in this plan").
- **Agent API:** the `disabled` arg of `plan.update` (`plan.get` shows `disabled`); [llm-api.md § DF106](../architecture/llm-api.md#df106-disabling-operations-and-features).
