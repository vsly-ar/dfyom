## Summary

One model serves all three cases: every setup after the first may carry **alignment points** – points, edges or holes that already exist on the stock when the setup starts (cut by an earlier setup, or a stock corner) – and earlier setups may cut **alignment aids** for them: plan-only operations that are not part of the design (a V-bit witness mark, a registration hole, a notch). Run mode's setup change gains an **alignment step**, exactly the user's flow: after the operator sets the work zero as today, the spindle (off) acts as a **pointer** – DFYOM moves it at the clearance height above one point ("this point must be right here"), the operator may jog Z down to sight the V-bit tip against the mark, adjusts the stock by hand, and steps previous/next through **all** points, the part zero included, until they all line up at once; then confirms. Nothing is measured or compensated: no program rotation, no coordinate-system rotation (GRBL has neither, and the user does not want them). Later phases add **probing** as an optional aid to the same physical check, **tiling** (one stock in several shifted setups of the same direction, toolpaths split by tile, the same pointer flow over aids in the overlap) and **split parts** (a design feature that cuts one part into pieces with margins and joints). Ranked reasons: [research § Recommendation](../research/2026-11-23-multi-setup-alignment.md#recommendation).

## Questions for the user

Each with a recommendation; "Q58 ok" takes it.

1. **Q58 – Phase 1 scope** (changed 2026-11-23). Phase 1 = flipped and turned setups of one stock only: alignment points on a setup, two kinds of aid (V-bit witness mark, registration hole), and the Run alignment step as you described it (zero first, then the spindle as a pointer at clearance height, jog Z, previous/next through all points including the zero, the operator adjusts the stock, confirm). No measurement, no compensation. Probing, tiling and split parts follow as phases 2–4. _Recommendation: yes._ It is the smallest slice you can use on a real flip, it needs no new toolpath geometry (a mark is a plunge or a short V-bit line, a hole is today's drilling), and every later phase reuses its model and its Run step.
2. **Q59 – Where aids live.** Alignment aids are **plan operations without a design operation** (a new `source: 'aid'` on the plan operation), added to a setup in Plan mode, kept across rebuilds (they do not come from the design), drawn in their own colour and named "Witness mark 1", "Registration hole 2". _Recommendation: yes._ The design stays the part; aids are about how it is made, like workholding ([WH-A](../modes/workholding.md#decisions)).
3. **Q60 – What may be an alignment point.** It must be geometry that exists when the setup starts: a stock corner or edge, a vertex, edge or hole of an operation in an **earlier setup** (in machining order), or an aid cut in an earlier setup; and it must be reachable from the setup's direction (on its up face, or an edge of it). Anything else is an error finding (`plan.alignment`, rules `notYetCut`, `notReachable`). _Recommendation: yes;_ this is your "it must be something that was already cut before", checked by the plan, not by the operator.
4. **Q61 – Zeroing and the cycle** (changed 2026-11-23). Your order: set the part zero first (jog to it, as today), then cycle the points. The **part zero is the first entry of the cycle**, so after nudging the stock the operator comes back to it and, if it moved, presses "Set zero here" (`G10 L20 P1 X0 Y0`, as today's zeroing); re-zeroing clears the ticks of the other points (Q63), because every pointer position follows the zero. The first draft's "zero from a reference" (zero so that a mark reads its expected coordinates) is **kept only as an optional convenience** for a stock corner that is unusable (sawn, chamfered, cut away): the operator zeroes on one alignment point instead of the corner, and that point takes the zero's place in the cycle. It is a different place to zero, not a correction, so it does not conflict with the physical flow. _Recommendation: yes; default to the stock-corner zero, offer "zero on this point" in the setup's alignment settings only._
5. **Q62 – The V-bit sequencing rule.** When a setup has witness marks for the next setup, its last tool is the marking V-bit, and the next setup's alignment step uses the same V-bit (still in the spindle) as the pointer before its first tool change; the plan suggests this order and the tool-change count shows the saving. _Recommendation: yes, as a suggestion (info finding `plan.alignment.markLast`), not a forced order;_ the user may already chamfer last.
6. **Q63 – The confirm gate** (changed 2026-11-23; the first draft's measured-error tolerance is withdrawn: without a probe the machine measures nothing, the operator sights). The operator ticks "lines up" at each point while the pointer is over it; "Confirm alignment" is enabled once every point, the zero included, is ticked since the last "Set zero here". Confirming with unticked points asks once ("2 of 4 points not checked. Continue anyway?"). _Recommendation: yes;_ it enforces your "all aligned at once" without pretending to measure.
7. **Q64 – Agents on a real machine.** Visiting points is motion: on a real connection the alignment step's moves and zero writes come from the UI only, as every motion command ([run.md § Safety 2](../modes/run.md#safety), Q50). Agents may add alignment points and aids, read the step's state (current point, ticks), and run the whole step on the virtual machine. _Recommendation: yes._
8. **Q65 – Compensation** – **withdrawn 2026-11-23.** The first draft proposed measuring the points and turning the program in DFYOM's post by the measured angle (GRBL has no `G68`). You do not want the program or the coordinate system rotated; the stock is turned to match instead. The analysis is kept as rejected in [research § Considered and rejected](../research/2026-11-23-multi-setup-alignment.md#considered-and-rejected-user-2026-11-23); no answer needed.
9. **Q66 – Probing (phase 2)** (changed 2026-11-23). An optional aid to the same physical flow, not compensation: at an edge or hole point the step offers "Probe", which runs a `G38.2` cycle (X/Y edge or corner with the touch plate – plate geometry as machine fields – or a 3D probe; hole centre with a 3D probe only) and shows which way and how far the stock is off, so the operator knows which way to nudge; then probes again. Nothing is written to the zero or the program. Needs the touch-plate work (hardening M4) merged. _Recommendation: yes, after phase 1;_ what is your probe today (none, a Z plate, an XYZ corner plate, a 3D probe)?
10. **Q67 – Tiling model (phase 3).** A tile is a **setup of the same direction with a stock shift** (the stock pushed along X or Y by a configured step) and a **window** (the part of the stock face the travel reaches, minus a safety margin). Each operation is split so every region is machined by exactly one tile (an ownership boundary in the middle of the overlap), with a small seam overlap for clearing; profiles are split at the boundary with lead-ins in the overlap; holes belong to the tile holding their centre. Aids in the overlap (marks or registration holes) are added automatically, and each tile is aligned by the same pointer flow over them. _Recommendation: yes;_ the overlap you configure is both the alignment zone and the reach margin.
11. **Q68 – Split parts (phase 4).** Cutting one part from several stocks is a **design** feature: split lines on a face cut the part into pieces, each gets its own stock with a configurable margin (default 5 mm per side) and a joint (butt with dowels, puzzle/dovetail key, finger, half-lap); a DFM warning flags features within a set distance of a split line. No Run alignment is needed. _Recommendation: yes, as the last phase;_ until then you design the pieces as separate parts.
12. **Q69 – Slot.** Phases 1–2 after the [v0.4 follow-up](v0.4-llm-first.md) (they need workholding's `base`, the spoilboard, for registration holes, and hardening's real-machine lane for the Run step), phase 3 after phase 1, phase 4 independent. _Recommendation: yes;_ phase 1's docs can be drafted now, on a branch.

## Exit criteria (per phase)

- **Phase 1:** a two-sided test part (pocket and holes on top, a pocket and chamfered edges on the bottom; flipped about Y) with two registration holes through into the spoilboard and two witness marks on the top's edges: the virtual machine runs both setups, and the alignment step moves the pointer to each point's expected machine position at the clearance height (zero first, previous/next wrapping, a re-zero clears the ticks); on the user's machine, after the stock is adjusted under the pointer, the bottom pocket lands within 0.3 mm of the top's holes (measured with calipers). Headless (MCP) and UI (hidden e2e) flows; `tools/list` within its budget.
- **Phase 2:** probing an edge and a hole on the virtual machine (simulated stock edges, the stock deliberately offset) reports the offset within 0.01 mm of the emulator's truth and changes neither G54 nor the program; on the user's probe, repeatability recorded.
- **Phase 3:** a 1200 × 300 mm sign on a machine with 800 mm X travel in two tiles: no region cut twice (simulated cut), the seam within 0.3 mm on the user's machine, every move inside the travel.
- **Phase 4:** a 600 × 400 mm part split into two pieces with a puzzle joint, each nested into its own stock with the margin; the pieces fit (user, by hand).

## Risks

| Risk                                                                                                           | Mitigation                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The spindle moves to a point while the operator's hands are at the stock (nudging it under the tip)            | Spindle off during the step (checked as in Probe Z); moves only on a click, at clearance height, then Z by jog only; Stop always reachable (Q64)                                                |
| Registration holes cut into a fixture plate or the machine bed                                                 | Depth into the base only with a sacrificial `base` (A6); capped depth; collision check against a fixture plate                                                                                  |
| Turning and nudging a clamped sheet by a fraction of a millimetre is fiddly, and clamping it again can move it | The step is not done until every point is ticked since the last zero (Q63): loosen, nudge, clamp, then cycle all points again; points spaced far apart make a small turn visible at the pointer |
| The operator trusts a stock corner that is not square or a sawn edge that is not straight                      | The cycle shows the cut marks against the zero, so a bad corner shows as a mark that cannot line up; optional zero on a point instead of the corner (Q61)                                       |
| Tiling seams show a step or a ridge                                                                            | Seam overlap (T2), every overlap point ticked before the tile runs, finishing passes owned by one tile where the seam crosses a visible face (doc 1.7 open question)                            |
| `tools/list` budget (13.2 K headroom after A8)                                                                 | Phase 1 ≤ 3 K: alignment points as one tool, aids through existing operation tools with a `source` arg (1.5)                                                                                    |
| Scope creep into a general in-process stock                                                                    | "Already cut" is decided from the machining order and surface ids, not from a simulated stock                                                                                                   |

## Open questions (for the docs, not the user)

- Edge alignment points on side setups: a mark on the edge between the top and the front is visible from both; is a mark on a face that is vertical in the new setup ever usable (only by its edge)?
- Does the alignment step belong to the setup-change prompt (one dialog) or to its own Run panel the prompt opens? (UI review with 1.3.)
- Registration holes for a flip need the flip axis fixed in the machine frame (pins in the spoilboard); how is the axis shown and checked when the setup's work zero corner moves to the other side after the flip?
- Tiling across a 90° turned setup (a stock longer than the travel in both X and Y): two-dimensional tile grids or only one axis first?
