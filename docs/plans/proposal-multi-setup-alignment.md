# Proposal: multi-setup alignment, tiling and split parts

Status: draft (proposed 2026-11-23; awaits the user's answers to [Q58–Q69](#questions-for-the-user); not scheduled) · Research: [research/2026-11-23-multi-setup-alignment.md](../research/2026-11-23-multi-setup-alignment.md) · Base: [plan-v0.2.md § Setups](../modes/plan-v0.2.md#setups-from-face-directions), [plan-v0.2.md § Setup change in Run](../modes/plan-v0.2.md#setup-change-in-run), [workholding.md](../modes/workholding.md) · Last updated: 2026-11-23

**Proposal, not a plan of record.** The user asked (2026-11-23, Telegram) to plan (1) repositioning a stock larger than the working area (tiling), (2) cutting one part from several stocks, both with tools for alignment and zeroing between setups that also serve the classical case of one stock turned or flipped. This file proposes phases, tasks and questions; nothing here starts before the user answers the questions and the docs of each phase are accepted. Once accepted it becomes a milestone in [roadmap.md](../roadmap.md) (or several) and this file is renamed to that plan. Question numbers continue after [hardening](hardening.md#questions-for-the-user)'s Q57 and are renumbered if another plan takes them first.

Status values: `done` · `doing` · `todo` · `blocked` (say on what) · `dropped` (say why).

## Summary

One model serves all three cases: every setup after the first may carry **alignment references** – points, edges or holes that already exist on the stock when the setup starts (cut by an earlier setup, or a stock corner) – and earlier setups may cut **alignment aids** for them: plan-only operations that are not part of the design (a V-bit witness mark, a registration hole, a notch). Run mode's setup change gains an **alignment step**: after the operator sets the work zero, DFYOM visits each reference at the clearance height, the operator jogs Z down, compares and nudges the stock (or reads the measured error), steps to the previous or next point, and confirms. Later phases measure the error and compensate it (translation by `G10 L20`, rotation by turning the program in DFYOM's post, since GRBL has no `G68`), probe references, and add **tiling** (one stock in several shifted setups of the same direction, toolpaths split by tile) and **split parts** (a design feature that cuts one part into pieces with margins and joints). Ranked reasons: [research § Recommendation](../research/2026-11-23-multi-setup-alignment.md#recommendation).

## Questions for the user

Each with a recommendation; "Q58 ok" takes it.

1. **Q58 – Phase 1 scope.** Phase 1 = flipped and turned setups of one stock only: alignment references on a setup, two kinds of aid (V-bit witness mark, registration hole), the Run alignment step **without compensation** (visit, jog Z, previous/next, show the measured error, confirm), and "zero from a reference". Tiling, compensation, probing and split parts follow as phases 2–5. _Recommendation: yes._ It is the smallest slice you can use on a real flip, it needs no new toolpath geometry (a mark is a plunge or a short V-bit line, a hole is today's drilling), and every later phase reuses its model and its Run step.
2. **Q59 – Where aids live.** Alignment aids are **plan operations without a design operation** (a new `source: 'aid'` on the plan operation), added to a setup in Plan mode, kept across rebuilds (they do not come from the design), drawn in their own colour and named "Witness mark 1", "Registration hole 2". _Recommendation: yes._ The design stays the part; aids are about how it is made, like workholding ([WH-A](../modes/workholding.md#decisions)).
3. **Q60 – What may be referenced.** A reference must be geometry that exists when the setup starts: a stock corner or edge, a vertex, edge or hole of an operation in an **earlier setup** (in machining order), or an aid cut in an earlier setup; and it must be reachable from the setup's direction (on its up face, or an edge of it). Anything else is an error finding (`plan.alignment`, rules `notYetCut`, `notReachable`). _Recommendation: yes;_ this is your "it must be something that was already cut before", checked by the plan, not by the operator.
4. **Q61 – Zeroing order.** You proposed: set the part zero first (jog to it), then visit the alignment points. Add a second way: **zero from a reference** – jog to the first reference (a mark or a hole you can see) and press "Set zero from this point": DFYOM writes `G10 L20 P1 X<x> Y<y>` with the reference's coordinates in the new setup, so the zero lands where the earlier setup's geometry says, not where the stock's (possibly uneven) corner is. _Recommendation: offer both; default to "zero from a reference" when the setup has one._ A cut reference is more accurate than a sawn stock corner.
5. **Q62 – The V-bit sequencing rule.** When a setup has witness marks for the next setup, its last tool is the marking V-bit, and the next setup's alignment step uses the same V-bit (still in the spindle) as a pointer before its first tool change; the plan suggests this order and the tool-change count shows the saving. _Recommendation: yes, as a suggestion (info finding `plan.alignment.markLast`), not a forced order;_ the user may already chamfer last.
6. **Q63 – Tolerance and the confirm gate.** The alignment step shows each point's measured error (the jogged position minus the expected one) and turns red above a per-setup **alignment tolerance** (default 0.2 mm). Confirming above it asks once ("Point 2 is off by 0.45 mm. Continue anyway?"). _Recommendation: yes;_ 0.2 mm is what a hobby router repeats and an eye at a V-bit tip resolves (research § B).
7. **Q64 – Agents on a real machine.** Visiting points is motion: on a real connection the alignment step's moves and zero writes come from the UI only, as every motion command ([run.md § Safety 2](../modes/run.md#safety), Q50). Agents may add references and aids, read the step's state and the measured errors, and run the whole step on the virtual machine. _Recommendation: yes._
8. **Q65 – Compensation (phase 2).** Measured offsets are applied by setting G54 (`G10 L20`); a measured rotation is applied by **turning the program in DFYOM's post** about the work zero (positions, arc centres; Z unchanged), with the soft-limit check rerun, a limit of ±5° and a residual check when there are three or more points. The turned program is a run artefact, never written back to the plan. _Recommendation: yes;_ GRBL 1.1 has no `G68`, and DFYOM is both post and sender, so this needs no controller support.
9. **Q66 – Probing (phase 3).** Probe references with `G38.2`: X/Y edges and corners with the touch plate (plate geometry as machine fields) or a 3D probe (tip diameter), hole centres with a 3D probe. Needs the touch-plate work (hardening M4) merged. _Recommendation: yes, after phase 2;_ what is your probe today (none, a Z plate, an XYZ corner plate, a 3D probe)?
10. **Q67 – Tiling model (phase 4).** A tile is a **setup of the same direction with a stock shift** (the stock pushed along X or Y by a configured step) and a **window** (the part of the stock face the travel reaches, minus a safety margin). Each operation is split so every region is machined by exactly one tile (an ownership boundary in the middle of the overlap), with a small seam overlap for clearing; profiles are split at the boundary with lead-ins in the overlap; holes belong to the tile holding their centre. Aids in the overlap (marks or registration holes) are added automatically. _Recommendation: yes;_ the overlap you configure is both the alignment zone and the reach margin.
11. **Q68 – Split parts (phase 5).** Cutting one part from several stocks is a **design** feature: split lines on a face cut the part into pieces, each gets its own stock with a configurable margin (default 5 mm per side) and a joint (butt with dowels, puzzle/dovetail key, finger, half-lap); a DFM warning flags features within a set distance of a split line. No Run alignment is needed. _Recommendation: yes, as the last phase;_ until then you design the pieces as separate parts.
12. **Q69 – Slot.** Phases 1–3 after the [v0.4 follow-up](v0.4-llm-first.md) (they need workholding's `base`, the spoilboard, for registration holes, and hardening's real-machine lane for the Run step), phase 4 after phase 2, phase 5 independent. _Recommendation: yes;_ phase 1's docs can be drafted now, on a branch.

## Exit criteria (per phase)

- **Phase 1:** a two-sided test part (pocket and holes on top, a pocket and chamfered edges on the bottom; flipped about Y) with two registration holes through into the spoilboard and two witness marks on the top's edges: the virtual machine runs both setups, the alignment step visits each reference at the clearance height and reports the error a deliberately shifted work zero produces; on the user's machine, the bottom pocket lands within 0.3 mm of the top's holes (measured with calipers). Headless (MCP) and UI (hidden e2e) flows; `tools/list` within its budget.
- **Phase 2:** the virtual machine with the stock deliberately turned by 1° (the emulator offsets the simulated plate/marks) gives a measured angle within 0.05°, and the turned program's cut lands on the earlier setup's geometry within 0.05 mm in the simulated cut.
- **Phase 3:** probing a corner and a hole on the virtual machine (simulated stock edges) sets G54 within 0.01 mm of the emulator's truth; on the user's probe, repeatability recorded.
- **Phase 4:** a 1200 × 300 mm sign on a machine with 800 mm X travel in two tiles: no region cut twice (simulated cut), the seam within the alignment tolerance on the user's machine, every move inside the travel.
- **Phase 5:** a 600 × 400 mm part split into two pieces with a puzzle joint, each nested into its own stock with the margin; the pieces fit (user, by hand).

## Risks

| Risk                                                                                                | Mitigation                                                                                                                                           |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The spindle moves to a point while the operator's hands are at the stock (nudging it under the tip) | Spindle off during the step (checked as in Probe Z); moves only on a click, at clearance height, then Z by jog only; Stop always reachable (Q64)     |
| Registration holes cut into a fixture plate or the machine bed                                      | Depth into the base only with a sacrificial `base` (A6); capped depth; collision check against a fixture plate                                       |
| A turned program leaves the travel or hits workholding                                              | Soft-limit check and collision check rerun on the turned program (C3); ±5° limit                                                                     |
| The operator trusts a stock corner that is not square or a sawn edge that is not straight           | Default to "zero from a reference" (Q61); a residual check with three points (C1)                                                                    |
| Tiling seams show a step or a ridge                                                                 | Seam overlap (T2), alignment tolerance in the step, finishing passes owned by one tile where the seam crosses a visible face (doc 1.8 open question) |
| `tools/list` budget (13.2 K headroom after A8)                                                      | Phase 1 ≤ 3 K: references as one tool, aids through existing operation tools with a `source` arg (1.5)                                               |
| Scope creep into a general in-process stock                                                         | "Already cut" is decided from the machining order and surface ids, not from a simulated stock                                                        |
